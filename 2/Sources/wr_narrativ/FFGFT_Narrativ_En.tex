\part{The Question}

\chapter{Do We Live in a Matrix?}

The question is old. Plato posed it with the allegory of the cave. Descartes as radical doubt. And in 2003 Nick Bostrom gave it a modern form: if advanced civilizations can run simulations of their ancestors and choose to do so, then we almost certainly live in such a simulation.

\begin{readerbox}
Imagine playing a video game so perfect that you cannot tell from inside whether it is a game. The physics would be consistent, the mathematics would work --- you would have no way to distinguish. That is Bostrom's argument.
\end{readerbox}

It is a serious question. And it is wrongly framed. ``Simulation'' is the wrong category --- it presupposes a simulator, an external instance.

What if the universe does not \textit{is} a simulation, but \textit{has} a mathematical structure --- deep, compelling, without external instance? What if the question is not: \textit{who computes us}, but: \textit{what is the structure the nature cannot avoid?}

What this book presents is such a structure: a compact four-dimensional torus $T^4$ with a threefold symmetry $\mathbb{Z}_3$, from which algebraically follows what must follow. No free parameters. No postulate about particles.
