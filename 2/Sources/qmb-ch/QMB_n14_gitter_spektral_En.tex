\section{Do the Lattices Become Flat?}

The FFGFT calculates geometrically on $T^4$ with the $D_4$ lattice and
quantum mechanically on $\mathcal{H} = L^2(T^4)\otimes\mathbb{C}^3$. During
the transition, an obvious question arises: Do the lattices become flat? Does anything remain
of kissing number~24, packing density, and triality, or is every lattice the same in the Hilbert space?

The answer: The torus is flat anyway --- curvature was never the
difference ---, and the lattice structure is not lost, but
\textbf{migrates into the spectrum and into the fiber}. In the Hilbert space, the
lattice becomes the index set of the Fourier modes. Thus it is no longer a sphere packing;
in this sense, every lattice becomes flat. But the information
does not disappear: The degeneracies of the Laplace operator are the
theta series of the lattice, the kissing number becomes the degeneracy of the first
excitation, the triality becomes the orbifold and fiber structure.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\text{Geometry}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Spectrum}\;+\;\text{Fiber}\;}
\end{equation}

Two anchors keep this translation stable. First, $D_4$ is self-dual up to
scaling: The dual lattice $D_4^*$ is again a
$D_4$ lattice, the Fourier transform does not lead out of the family.
Second, the fiber changes nothing about the lattice: The
$\mathbb{C}^3$ fiber hangs at every point and carries the
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ structure, the mode lattice remains the dual lattice.

\textbf{On notation.} In lattice theory, $\Lambda$ is the usual
symbol for a lattice. In the FFGFT corpus, $\Lambda$ is occupied (cosmological
constant, closure scale). This chapter therefore uses $\Gamma$
for the lattice and $\Gamma^*$ for the dual lattice; the edge lengths of the

their ratio $r = R_4/R_{123}$.

\section{The Reciprocity Is Exact Mode-by-Mode --- as a Position Relation}

The fourth direction is the time-mass dual; it is unrolled onto time.
Two test questions: How do time periods arise, and does $\tilde T\cdot m = 1$
hold only on average?

The unrolling of the compact fourth direction is the universal
covering $\mathbb{R}\to S^1$; the deck transformation group is
$\mathbb{Z}$, the unrolled coordinate counts cycle traversals. A
$k_4$ mode thereby becomes a periodic function with period
$2\pi R_4/k_4$. Since $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ is abelian, this
bookkeeping is lossless.

winding number is the mass number.} Thus
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_4\cdot m = \frac{2\pi R_4}{k_4}\cdot\frac{k_4}{R_4} = 2\pi
  \qquad\text{exact, for every mode.}
\end{equation}
The reciprocity holds not only on average --- it is identically fulfilled mode-by-mode, because period and mass are built from the same quantum number and
the same radius.\status{K}

\textbf{But the period is initially a position, not a duration.} The quantity
$\lambda_4 = 2\pi R_4/k_4$ is an arc length on the circle: the
distance in $x_4$ after which the mode repeats. The relation
$\lambda_4\cdot m = 2\pi$ is the de Broglie relation on the
mass direction --- wavelength times wavenumber ---, not a time statement.
Duration arises only from the unrolled coordinate: A point of the
unrolled time is a pair $t = w\cdot2\pi R_4+x_4$ of revolution number
$w\in\mathbb{Z}$ and position on the cycle. The duration is entirely
in the numerator $w$, the position in the remainder. On the cycle there is no
distinguished beginning; every moment is a place that recurs.

\textbf{The temporal reading carries a $\gamma$ factor.} If one asks
about the oscillation period in the unrolled time, then
$T_{\mathrm{osc}} = 2\pi/E$ with $E = \sqrt{p^2+m^2}$ is responsible, and then
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{osc}}\cdot m = \frac{2\pi}{\gamma}\le2\pi,
  \qquad\text{equality exactly at } p_{\mathrm{space}} = 0.
\end{equation}
Exactly $2\pi$ is the position reading; the time reading coincides with it only for the
resting mode.

\textbf{In the mixture, the equation becomes an inequality.} If a state
superposes several $k_4$ shells with weights $p_k$, then
$\langle T\rangle\langle m\rangle = 2\pi\langle k\rangle\langle1/k\rangle$
and by Jensen $\langle k\rangle\langle1/k\rangle\ge1$, with equality
exactly when a single shell is occupied. Numerically: sharp shell exactly

$k = 1\dots50$ yields $2{,}295$; thermal distributions between
$1{,}00002$ and $2{,}6$; two thousand random distributions never below~1.
The conjecture reverses: The condition for $\tilde T\cdot m = 1$ is
not uniform distribution, but \textbf{sharpness} --- exactly one occupied
mass shell. The excess measures the mass dispersion of the state.

Thus three statements stand in three directions: The position reading is
always $2\pi$; the time reading of a moving mode is $2\pi/\gamma$;
the mass mixture lies at $\ge2\pi$. The exact relation holds for the
resting, sharp mode --- there and only there do both
correction factors vanish simultaneously. Motion pushes the product down,
mass dispersion raises it. This is not a contradiction to the native
FFGFT formulation, but the Kaluza-Klein reading of its
validity conditions.
