\chapter{Bell's Theorem}
\label{ch:bell}

\section{The Fundamental Question}

Bell tests check whether the correlations of entangled particles can be explained by
a local-realistic theory — a theory
in which each particle has properties defined from the beginning
that are measured independently of the other particle.

John Bell showed in 1964 that no such theory can reproduce the
quantum predictions.\status{E} The argument is
mathematically sharp and cannot be circumvented by technical refinements.

\section{The CHSH Inequality}

In the form formulated by Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt
(CHSH, 1969), the Bell inequality reads:\status{E}
\begin{equation}
  |E(a,b)-E(a,c)| + |E(a',b)+E(a',c)| \le 2,
  \label{eq:chsh}
\end{equation}
where $E(a,b)$ denotes the correlation of the measurement results at the
detector angles $a$ and $b$.

Quantum mechanics violates this bound up to the
Tsirelson bound $2\sqrt2\approx2{,}828$.\status{E} All
loophole-free experiments since 2015 confirm the
QM prediction and refute local-realistic theories.\status{E}

\section{The Three Assumptions}

Bell's proof assumes three assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Locality:} The result at detector A does not depend
        on the setting at detector B.
  \item \textbf{Realism:} The measurement results are predetermined
        (by hidden variables).
  \item \textbf{Freedom of choice:} The detector settings are chosen
        independently of the hidden variables.
\end{enumerate}
At least one of these assumptions is false — that is Bell's
achievement. QM gives up locality and realism together.

\section{Historical FFGFT Approaches and Their Current Status}

The early FFGFT documents (Doc.~023, 023a, 074, autumn 2025)
attempted various ways to reconcile the violation with local reality:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Time field damping} (Doc.~023): The correlations
        are attenuated by a $\xi$-dependent factor.
        Result: CHSH reduction from $2{,}828$ to $2{,}827$ —
        too small for today's experiments.\status{S}
  \item \textbf{Superdeterminism} (Doc.~074): The freedom of choice
        is violated because the energy field jointly determines detector settings
        and particle properties. This path
        is superseded by Doc.~230 (see Chapter~\ref{ch:bell_ffgft}).
  \item \textbf{Topological connection} (Doc.~230, May 2026):
        No concession to the assumptions necessary — the common
        carrier makes the correlation a geometric
        property. This path is authoritative today.\status{B}
\end{itemize}
