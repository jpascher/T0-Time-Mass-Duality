\section{States as Mode Configurations}

In standard QM, a state is a vector $|\psi\rangle$ in a
Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. In the FFGFT, a state is a
mode configuration $(z, r, \theta)$ on the carrier $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$.
The connection is bijective: The spectral operator
\begin{equation}
  \hat F = \sum_k f_k\, |k\rangle\langle k|,
  \label{eq:spektral}
\end{equation}
with modes $k$ from the lattice $D_4$ is self-adjoint on
$\mathcal{H} = L^2(T^4)\otimes\mathbb{C}^3$.\status{B} (Doc.~327)
Every QM calculation can be mapped into FFGFT language and vice versa;
the formalisms yield the same statistical predictions.\status{B}

\section{Measurement Without the Collapse Postulate}

The collapse of the wave function is a postulate in standard QM
without a mechanical explanation. In the FFGFT, measurement is a
\emph{geometric interaction}: The measuring device drives the mode
to the nearest stable extremum ($z=+1$ or $z=-1$). This is
a deterministic movement in mode space — but because of the
unknown initial phase $\theta$ and smallest variations of the initial conditions, statistically indistinguishable
from Born rule behavior:\status{B}
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{FFGFT}}(z=+1) = \frac{1+z}{2} = |\alpha|^2 = P_{\mathrm{QM}}(0).
\end{equation}
The two formalisms yield identical statistical predictions
(Doc.~230). Interpretively they differ: What QM describes as chance
is, in the FFGFT, ignorance of the exact carrier configuration.

\section{The Absence of the Time Operator}

Pauli's objection shows that for a Hamiltonian bounded from below, there cannot be a self-adjoint, canonically conjugate
time operator.\status{E} Time is missing in the Hilbert space — not as an oversight,
but structurally.

In the FFGFT, this is not a deficiency: $\tilde T$ is not an operator, but
a mode-bound property of the carrier. For mode $i$,
$\tilde T_i = 1/m_i$; different modes carry different time scales.
Superposition of modes of different mass thus results in
\emph{incompatible time scales} — a structural statement about the
limits of cross-mode superposition.\status{B} (Doc.~334)

The Schrödinger equation remains fully valid as an effective description; the time $t$ in it is the global coordinate that is unrolled from the
carrier (Type I pullback, Doc.~333).

\begin{laierspur}
That QM has no time operator sounds technical — but it means:
The theory can say nothing about the duration of processes, only about
states at fixed points in time. The FFGFT makes this explicit: Each
particle has its own internal clock ($\tilde T = 1/m$). Two particles
of different mass literally vibrate in different rhythms.
\end{laierspur}
