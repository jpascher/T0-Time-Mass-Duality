\section{What Entanglement Is in the FFGFT}

Two qubits are called entangled if their joint state cannot be written as a
product $|\psi_1\rangle\otimes|\psi_2\rangle$.
The maximally entangled Bell state
\begin{equation}
  |\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)
\end{equation}
has a geometric characterization in the FFGFT: The two modes
share a common carrier $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$. Their
$(z_i,r_i,\theta_i)$ coordinates are not independent --- they are
knots of the same topological configuration that cannot be reduced to product form.\status{B}

\textbf{Entanglement in the FFGFT is a topological connection,
not a nonlocal correlation.} The particles never had two separate
carriers; they share the same one from the beginning. What QM describes as action at a distance is a property of the common geometry.

\section{EPR and Locality}

The EPR paradox (1935): If QM is complete, entangled
particles are nonlocally connected; Einstein considered this unacceptable and
postulated hidden variables.\status{E}

The FFGFT resolves the paradox geometrically. Locality holds on the torus
in the sense that no signal is transmitted faster than light. The
correlation is a property of the common geometry, not a signal transmission. What is given up is only the idea that each particle would have an \emph{independent} carrier --- and that
is not observable.\status{B}

\section{Apparent Instantaneity: Not a Physical Problem}

The collapse of the wave function appears to happen instantaneously: One measures
qubit A and B immediately knows which state it is in, no matter how far
away. This seems to contradict the theory of relativity.

Before explaining the FFGFT resolution, a step back is worthwhile. In
standard mathematics, arithmetic operations are considered instantaneous: If
$2 + 2 = 4$, then that holds simultaneously for all places in the expression
--- no one associates a physical instantaneity with this that would be causally problematic. The equality is a structural
statement about the object, not an event in space. The same applies
to the state description in QM: The wave function
$|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)$ is a
mathematical description of a state --- not a process that propagates
anywhere. The instantaneity of the descriptive adjustment after
the measurement is mathematical, not physical in nature.

In the FFGFT, the same argument applies at the level of the carrier: The
relation $\tilde T\cdot m = 1$ is a \textbf{structural
property} of each mode on $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$ --- analogous to the fact that
an equation holds simultaneously for all its variables. It is a
\textbf{local constraint} at the same spatial point, not a
signal between distant points.

The resolution comes from the separation of two levels: A change at $\mathbf{x}_1$ then propagates via
the dynamic field equations:\status{B}
\begin{equation}
  \text{Change at }\mathbf{x}_1
  \to \text{propagation with }v\le c
  \to \text{effect at }\mathbf{x}_2.
\end{equation}
The time delay is $\Delta t = |\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_1|/c$.

Local adjustment and global propagation are two different processes.
The local adjustment ($T\cdot m = 1$ at the same point) occurs on the
Planck time scale $t_P\sim10^{-43}$\,s --- practically instantaneous for every
measurable time scale. The propagation to distant points is limited by the
speed of light. This hierarchy explains why many
quantum processes appear to be instantaneous.

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial^2\tilde T}{\partial t^2} = c^2\nabla^2\tilde T + Q(\tilde T,m,\rho),
\end{equation}
with the source function $Q = -4\pi G\rho\cdot\tilde T$. This is a
hyperbolic differential equation, characteristic of
wave propagation with finite speed.

The causal structure is manifested by the Green's function:
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}',t-t') = \theta(t-t')\cdot
  \frac{\delta(|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'|-c(t-t'))}{4\pi|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'|}.
\end{equation}
The Heaviside function $\theta(t-t')$ guarantees causality: No
effect before its cause. The delta function encodes propagation with the
speed of light. This structure is fully compatible with the special
theory of relativity.\status{B}

A concrete example: If $m(\mathbf{x}_0)$ changes at $t = 0$, then
$\tilde T(\mathbf{x}_0)$ adjusts immediately (local constraint);
at the same time $\nabla^2\tilde T\ne0$ arises, which generates a field disturbance;
this propagates as a wave with $v = c$ and reaches
$\mathbf{x}_1$ at time $t = r/c$.
