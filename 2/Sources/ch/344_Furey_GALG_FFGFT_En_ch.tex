Doug Matzke's IPI mail of 3~September 2026 presents concrete GALG results on Furey fermions
in Cl$(6)$: complete Up/Down fermion sets Su/Sd, idempotents $N_1,N_2,N_3$, tripotent $N$,

\section{Theorem A --- $N_k$ are idempotents in characteristic 3}

$N_k = -1 + (-i\,B_k)$, $B_k^2 = -1$ (Witt pair):
\[
N_k^2 = 1 + 2i\,B_k + (-1)(-1) = 2 + 2i\,B_k \equiv -1 + (-i\,B_k) \pmod{3} = N_k. \quad \BM
\]
The sum loses its scalar part since $-3\equiv0$: $N_1+N_2+N_3 \equiv (-i)\sum B_k = N$.

\subsection*{Why are the neutrino and the vacuum the same?}

Three independent routes lead to the same result:

\textbf{1. Furey/GALG (this theorem).}
Vacuum state Vss[0] is defined as the object annihilated by all creation operators:
no Fock-space content, no quantum numbers. The neutrino Su[0] has the same
algebraic structure: no colour, no electric charge, only minimal weak coupling.
Hence they are identical.

\textbf{2. Galois/FFGFT (Doc.~339/343).}
GF$(27)^*$ has a generator $g$ of order~26.
Orbit $O_1=\{g^1,g^3,g^9\}$ is the orbit of the generator itself ---
the algebraically simplest object in the structure. $\nu_1$ lies in $O_1$.
The vacuum Vss[0] is the state with zero torus winding ($n=0$).
Smallest mass $\leftrightarrow$ simplest orbit $\leftrightarrow$ $n=0$ state ---
three names for the same minimality property.

\textbf{3. Photon analogy (Doc.~007).}
$m_\nu = \xi^2/2 \times m_e = 4.54$~meV. The photon has mass~0
and is the limiting case of the vacuum. The neutrino is the massive particle
closest to the vacuum: double $\xi$ suppression = maximum approach to $m=0$
among all massive fermions \KM.

\textbf{Structural statement.}
In a theory that derives all particles from an algebraic structure, the lightest
massive fermion must lie closest to the vacuum state.
Neutrino and vacuum are the algebraic minimum: the same object seen from
different directions \BM.

\section{Theorem G --- Anti-neutrinos: Dirac or Majorana?}

\textbf{The tension.} In GALG/Furey (Theorem~C) the neutrino is
$\mathrm{Su}[0]=\mathrm{Vss}[0]$ and the anti-neutrino is $\mathrm{Sd}[0]=\mathrm{cc}(\mathrm{Su}[0])=\mathrm{Vss}[1]\neq\mathrm{Vss}[0]$.
Hence neutrinos in Furey's construction are \textbf{Dirac particles} ($\nu\neq\bar\nu$).

In FFGFT (Doc.~343, Theorem~D$'''$), $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$ lie in orbits $O_1$ and $O_3$,
which are self-inverse under $k\mapsto k^{-1}$ --- the \emph{algebraic} Majorana criterion.

\begin{theorem}[Clarification of Majorana concept \BM/\SM]
Two distinct Majorana concepts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Algebraic \BM:} Orbit $O_k$ is self-inverse under $k\mapsto k^{-1}$ mod~13
--- a statement about GF$(27)^*$ structure, not about spin.
\item \textbf{Physical \SM:} $\nu=\bar\nu$ in the Lorentz sense (Majorana condition).
This requires Sd[0]=Su[0], which does not hold in GALG/Furey.
\end{enumerate}
The Galois algebra fixes the algebraic property \BM; the physical Majorana
condition is an additional assumption \SM.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Position of anti-neutrinos \BM/\SM.}
GALG: $\bar\nu=$ Sd[0] $=$ Vss[1] in the odd vacuum (sigma-orbit, other sector, Doc.~339).
FFGFT: the anti-neutrino would lie in the negation $f_1\leftrightarrow g_1$
(Doc.~343, Theorem~D$''$) --- order-13 class $g_1$, other sector.
Both frameworks place the anti-neutrino in the structurally ``other'' sector.

\textbf{Consequence for $m_{ee}$.}
The prediction $m_{ee}\approx7.1$~meV from Doc.~340 assumes physical Majorana neutrinos
$\nu_{1,2}$. If $\nu_1,\nu_2$ are instead Dirac particles (as in Furey), $m_{ee}$ vanishes entirely.
The $m_{ee}$ prediction is conditioned on the Majorana assumption \SM.

\section{Theorem H --- The Galois algebra forces Dirac neutrinos}

\begin{theorem}[Dirac constraint \BM\ under assumption \SM]
If the sector pairing $k \mapsto -k$ in GF$(27)^*$ corresponds to the physical
charge conjugation~$C$, then \textbf{all} neutrinos are Dirac particles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A Majorana particle is a fixed point of charge conjugation: $\nu=\bar\nu$,
i.e.\ class $f_i$ must map to itself under~$C$.

Under the sector pairing $k \mapsto -k \pmod{26}$:
\begin{align}
f_1=\{1,3,9\} &\;\mapsto\; \{25,23,17\} = f_2 \neq f_1 \notag\\
f_2=\{17,23,25\} &\;\mapsto\; f_1 \neq f_2 \notag\\
f_3=\{5,15,19\} &\;\mapsto\; f_4 \neq f_3 \notag\\
f_4=\{7,11,21\} &\;\mapsto\; f_3 \neq f_4 \notag
\end{align}

No primitive class is a fixed point of the sector pairing \BM.
A Majorana fixed point would require $f_i \mapsto f_i$ --- this does not exist
in GF$(27)^*$. The anti-particle of the neutrino $f_1$ is $f_2$: $\nu_1 \neq \bar\nu_1$. \qquad$\square$
\end{proof}

\textbf{Assumption \SM.} The argument holds if $k\mapsto-k$ in GF$(27)^*$
corresponds to physical charge conjugation~$C$. In Doc.~336 the Frobenius
involution $x\mapsto x^3$ is identified with the sector separation; the
identification with~$C$ is physically motivated there but not algebraically proved.

\textbf{Consistency \BM.} In GALG/Furey (Doc.~344, Theorem~C):
$\mathrm{Sd}[0]=\mathrm{Vss}[1]\neq\mathrm{Vss}[0]=\mathrm{Su}[0]$,
i.e.\ $\bar\nu\neq\nu$ --- fully consistent with this theorem.


\subsection*{Plain language: What is $m_{ee}$ and why does it matter?}

\textbf{The process.} A radioactive nucleus converts two neutrons into two protons
simultaneously, emitting two electrons. In ordinary double beta decay ($2\nu\beta\beta$)
two anti-neutrinos also come out --- the energy is shared among four particles.
In \emph{neutrinoless} decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) no anti-neutrinos come out.

\textbf{Why this requires Majorana.} The anti-neutrino produced at the first
nuclear vertex must be absorbed as a neutrino at the second vertex. This is only
possible if neutrino and anti-neutrino are the same particle ($\nu = \bar\nu$,
Majorana condition). For Dirac neutrinos ($\nu \neq \bar\nu$) the process is
exactly forbidden.

\textbf{What $m_{ee}$ measures.} The half-life of $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay is
proportional to $1/m_{ee}^2$, where
\[
  m_{ee} = \left| m_1 U_{e1}^2 + m_2 U_{e2}^2 + m_3 U_{e3}^2 \right|
\]
combines all three neutrino masses weighted by PMNS mixing elements and Majorana phases.

\textbf{Why interference matters.} The three terms can add (constructively) or
partially cancel (destructively) depending on the Majorana phases. With FFGFT
masses from Doc.~340 and phases $\approx 0$: constructive gives
$m_{ee} \approx 7.1$~meV; destructive gives $m_{ee} \approx 0.12$~meV;
Dirac gives $m_{ee} = 0$ exactly.

\textbf{What nEXO can measure.} nEXO targets sensitivity $\sim5$~meV --- directly
in the decision range. A signal at $\sim7$~meV confirms Majorana and refutes
Theorem~H; no signal down to $5$~meV excludes the constructive Majorana prediction
and is consistent with Dirac or destructive interference.

\subsection*{Consequence: $m_{ee}=0$ and no neutrinoless double beta decay}

For Dirac neutrinos, neutrinoless double beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) is
\textbf{exactly forbidden}:
\[
  m_{ee} = \left|\sum_i m_i\,U_{ei}^2\right| \equiv 0.
\]

\subsection*{Why there are no further particles --- \BM}

This is one of the strongest statements: the algebra prescribes not only
what exists, but also what cannot exist.

\textbf{Four primitive classes, no more.}
GF$(27)^*$ has exactly $\varphi(26)=12$ primitive elements forming exactly
4 Frobenius three-cycles. There is no fifth primitive class.

\textbf{Two layers, no more.}
The sector pairing $k\mapsto-k$ pairs $f_1\leftrightarrow f_2$ and
$f_3\leftrightarrow f_4$ --- exactly two pairs, no remainder.

\textbf{Three generations, not four.}
Each primitive class is a three-cycle.
3 elements per orbit = 3 generations.
Forced by the Frobenius orbit length, not chosen.

\textbf{16 fermions per generation, complete (GALG/Furey).}
$8+8$ = Su $\cup$ Sd covers exactly one generation of the Standard Model:
neutrino, 3 colours $\times$ up-quark, 3 colours $\times$ down-quark,

The Majorana layer $\{f_1,f_2\}$ is fully occupied by $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$.
