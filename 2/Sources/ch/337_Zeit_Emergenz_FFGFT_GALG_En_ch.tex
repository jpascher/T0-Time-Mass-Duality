\chapter*{Time Emergence and Gravitational Constant: Two Suggestions for Matzke\textquotesingle s GALG Framework}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Two Suggestions for Matzke\textquotesingle s GALG Framework}

\begin{abstract}
This document offers two suggestions for Doug Matzke's GALG
framework~\cite{Matzke2002,Matzke2026IPI}, supported by direct quotations
and algebraic verification.

\textbf{Proposal~1 (time):}
Matzke's co-exclusion primitive generates proto-time through operator
change~\cite{Matzke2002}~(p.~37), in direct parallel to FFGFT
decompactification (unrolling, Doc.~333). Both frameworks share the same
three-layer emergence structure. This is not a weakness of GALG but an
unspoken parallel that strengthens it.

\textbf{Proposal~2 (G):}
The Schwarzschild derivation in~\cite{Matzke2026IPI} starts from the
hyperbit $e_i^2 = +1$ --- a purely algebraic, G-free initial condition.
The SI interface is introduced only through $T_\text{Planck}$, from which
$M_\text{bit} = m_P\cdot\ln2$ follows necessarily. Since $m_P = \sqrt{\hbar c/G}$,
this interface contains~$G$ --- but this is structurally unavoidable:
any theory that produces SI units must build a bridge to measured constants
somewhere. $T_\text{Planck}$ is that bridge, and it is legitimate.
The suggestion: restrict the claim \emph{``without free parameters''}
to the algebraic layer $e_i^2=+1\to\text{Cl}(6)$, and name $T_\text{Planck}$
explicitly as the SI interface.
In FFGFT, $\xi$ and $m_e$ serve as the SI interface (Doc.~012) ---
a different route, but the same structural necessity.

Both proposals are documented through direct quotations from Matzke's
publications and verified by Python scripts.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{The basic problem.}
Observable time is a macroscopic phenomenon. Quantum mechanics and
algebraic field theories operate at a level where time is either
absent or appears only as a discrete primitive.
How does continuous, observable time arise from a timeless algebraic
base structure?

\textbf{What this document shows.}
Two suggestions, supported by
direct quotations from his own publications:
(1)~Time emergence in GALG has the same structure as in FFGFT ---
this strengthens Doug\textquotesingle s framework but has not yet been made explicit.
(2)~The G derivation in~\cite{Matzke2026IPI} is algebraically necessary
from the SI interface $T_\text{Planck}$ onward --- the claim
\emph{``without free parameters''} holds precisely for the algebraic layer,
not for the SI embedding.

\section{Time in FFGFT: compactified and emergent}
% ============================================================

\subsection{The compactified time scale $\tilde{T} = 1/m$}

In FFGFT, each particle of mass $m$ carries an intrinsic
time scale (Doc.~321~\cite{Pascher321}):
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{T} = \frac{1}{m}
  \qquad \text{(natural units, $\hbar = c = 1$)}
\end{equation}
This time scale lives on the compact geometry T$^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$.
It is mode-specific --- each mode has its own time scale,
and cross-mode superpositions generate incompatible time scales
(Doc.~334~\cite{Pascher334}) \BM.

\subsection{Decompactification as the emergence mechanism}

The compact dimension is not directly observable.
Observable time arises only through unrolling:
the transition from compact geometry to macroscopic spacetime
(Doc.~333~\cite{Pascher333}).

Doc.~333 distinguishes two unrolling types:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Type~I} (measurement act): lossy, irreversible ---
    wavefunction collapse as decompactification.
  \item \textbf{Type~III} (pullback): lossless, reversible ---
    geometric retraction to the compact manifold.
\end{itemize}
Observable time is a Type-I phenomenon: it arises through
the irreversible unrolling act, not from the compact geometry itself.

\textbf{Core statement FFGFT} \BM:
Before decompactification, only $\tilde{T} = 1/m$ exists
as a mode-bound geometric quantity.
Classical, measurable time $t$ is the result of unrolling.

\section{Time in GALG: proto-temporal and emergent}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Co-occurrence and co-exclusion}

Matzke~\cite{Matzke2002} defines two fundamental primitives
for geometric algebra (Ch.~4.2, pp.~36--39):

\textbf{Co-occurrence} (spatial primitive): two states exist
simultaneously, expressed as GA addition $a+b$.
Direct quote~\cite{Matzke2002}, p.~37:
\begin{quote}
``Co-occurrence is defined as two or more states `occurring'
simultaneously [...] commutative properties of addition reflects
the lack of temporal ordering characteristic of simultaneity.''
\end{quote}
Co-occurrence is static --- no temporal ordering.

\textbf{Co-exclusion} (temporal primitive): an operator changes
the state through GA multiplication $ab$.
Direct quote~\cite{Matzke2002}, p.~37:
\begin{quote}
``Since co-exclusion infers an operator for change and implies
temporal sequence, it is considered to be the temporal primitive.
Conversely, co-occurrence is labeled the spatial primitive
since it is static.''
\end{quote}

\subsection{Proto-time emerges from operators}

Classical time is not encoded in the GALG framework,
but emerges from the interaction of disjoint operators.
Direct quote~\cite{Matzke2002}, p.~14:
\begin{quote}
``Interaction of disjoint finite sets of dimensions
(operators and resulting co-exclusions) creates a
synchronization-based proto-time from which classical time
and energy metrics ultimately emerge.''
\end{quote}

On the need for a different temporal concept in the quantum domain
(p.~12):
\begin{quote}
``The quantum domain must have a different kind of primitive
temporal framework, if for no other reason than it exists in
a completely different spatial environment. Conceptually, this
proto-temporal framework is identical to the synchronization
tokens and mechanisms used by asynchronous logic.''
\end{quote}

\textbf{Core statement GALG}:
Before emergence, only the algebraic primitive of co-exclusion
(multiplication) exists.
Classical time and energy arise from the interaction of
disjoint operator sets.

\section{Open question}
% ============================================================

Is Matzke's co-exclusion the algebraic expression of the same
structure that FFGFT describes as decompactification?

Concretely: in FFGFT, unrolling Type~I (measurement act, collapse)
is an irreversible transition --- just as co-exclusion describes
a state change through an operator without possibility of reversal
(Matzke~\cite{Matzke2002}, p.~145:
\textit{``The process is irreversible because a multiplicative
inverse does not exist for $(1\pm a_0)$ and $(1\pm a_1)$''}).

This parallel --- irreversibility as a shared feature ---
could be the algebraic core of the connection~\SM.

Verification would require:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Explicit construction of the unrolling operator
    in GALG notation.
  \item Proof that co-exclusion $+$ irreducibility reproduces
    the FFGFT mass formula $\tilde{T}=1/m$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mass and gravitational constant: derived or implicit?}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Matzke's Schwarzschild derivation and the implicit G}

In his IPI Letters paper 2026~\cite{Matzke2026IPI}, Matzke presents the derivation as follows:
\begin{quote}
``This paper derives the complete black hole picture ---
Schwarzschild radius, Bekenstein entropy, Hawking radiation,
collapse threshold, and information conservation --- from a single
algebraic axiom with no general relativity and no free parameters.''
\end{quote}

The derivation uses $M_\text{bit} = m_P \cdot \ln 2 / (2\pi)$
as starting point (Landauer at $T_\text{Planck}$, then $E = mc^2$).
From the collapse condition Compton = Schwarzschild:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\hbar}{M_\text{coll}\,c} = \frac{2G\,M_\text{coll}}{c^2}
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  G = \frac{\hbar c}{2\,M_\text{coll}^2}
\end{equation}
with $M_\text{coll} = n_\text{thresh}\cdot M_\text{bit} = m_P/\sqrt{2}$
(numerically verified: 0.00\% deviation).

The implicit G from this construction:
\begin{equation}
  G_\text{doug} = \frac{\hbar c}{2\,M_\text{coll}^2}
  = \frac{\hbar c}{m_P^2}
  = 6.6743\times10^{-11}\;\text{m}^3/(\text{kg}\cdot\text{s}^2)
\end{equation}
Deviation from experimental value: $0.00\,\%$.

\subsection{The SI interface and what it achieves}

Matzke's initial condition is purely algebraic and G-free:
\begin{equation}
  e_i^2 = +1 \quad\text{(hyperbit, $+1$ signature)}
\end{equation}
From this follows the Witt decomposition into nilpotents $\{ap, an\}$
with $ap^2=0$, then Cl(6), then the Planck voxel --- all without~$G$.

The SI interface enters only through $T_\text{Planck}$:
\begin{equation}
  M_\text{bit} = \frac{k_B\,T_P\,\ln 2}{c^2} = m_P\,\ln 2
\end{equation}
Since $m_P = \sqrt{\hbar c/G}$, this interface contains~$G$ ---
but this is structurally \textbf{unavoidable}: any theory producing
dimensionful SI quantities must insert measured constants somewhere.
$T_\text{Planck}$ is the minimal such bridge.

Matzke himself names this clearly in his IPI mail of 27~Aug~2026:
\begin{quote}
``the fact that $M_\text{bit}$ is directly related to $M_\text{Planck}$
was a happy coincident based on Planck units consolidation only.''
\end{quote}

The suggestion: restrict the claim \emph{``without free parameters''}
explicitly to the algebraic layer $e_i^2=+1\to\text{Cl}(6)$.
The SI embedding via $T_\text{Planck}$ is legitimate and necessary ---
but should be named as such, not presented as part of the
parameter-free derivation.

\subsection{FFGFT: G as genuine derivation}

In FFGFT, $G$ is derived from $\xi$ and $m_e$ (Doc.~012~\cite{Pascher012}),
\emph{without} $m_P$ as input:
\begin{equation}
  G = \frac{\xi^2\,\hbar c}{4\,m_e^2}\cdot K_\text{frak}
  \qquad \text{with } \xi = \frac{4}{30\,000},\; K_\text{frak} = \frac{74}{75}
\end{equation}
Here $\xi$ is fixed by the T$^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$ geometry and $m_e$ by the
mass formula, both independent of $G$.
This is a genuine derivation: $G$ is output, not input.

Both frameworks recover the same numerical value for $G$,
and both require an SI interface.
GALG chooses $T_\text{Planck}$ --- minimal and physically well motivated.

Both routes are legitimate; the difference is the choice of bridge,
not whether a bridge is needed.
