\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The Zeta Function of the T$^4$/Z$_3$ Torus}

\begin{abstract}
The Galois factorisation classes of Doc.~342 and the harmonic series on the
T$^4$ torus of Doc.~159 together yield a precise statement about the zeta
function of the FFGFT spacetime.

Four results:
\textbf{(A)}~The Z$_3$ orbifold projection of T$^4$/Z$_3$ transforms the full
Riemann zeta into a Dirichlet L-function:
$\zeta_{T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3}(s) = L(s,\chi_0) = (1-3^{-s})\,\zeta(s)$ \BM.
\textbf{(B)}~The Euler product of this L-function is hierarchically ordered by
the entry depth $k = \mathrm{ord}_p(3)$; the physically visible primes
$\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}$ are exactly those with $k \leq 6$ \BM.
\textbf{(C)}~The orbifold projection generates additional zeros at
$s = 2\pi i k/\ln 3$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$) on the imaginary
axis $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 0$; these are the algebraic signature of the Z$_3$
sector separation \BM.
\textbf{(D)}~The four Frobenius conjugacy classes of primitive elements in
GF$(27)^*$ (Doc.~342, Theorem~A) correspond to four Hecke L-functions for
the characters of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$;
whether these four classes carry distinct mass layers remains open \SM.

Check script: \texttt{pruef\_343\_zeta\_galois.py}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Theorem A: Orbifold projection and Dirichlet L-function}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Decomposition of the zeta by Z$_3$ classes}

On T$^4$/Z$_3$ the allowed modes $n \in \mathbb{Z}^4$ are structured by
Z$_3$ invariance. In the one-dimensional analogue $\zeta(s)$ splits into
three partial sums by $n \bmod 3$ \BM:
\begin{equation}
  \zeta(s) = \underbrace{\sum_{\substack{n=1\\3\mid n}}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}}_{= 3^{-s}\zeta(s)}
  + \underbrace{\sum_{\substack{n=1\\n\equiv 1(3)}}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}}_{L_1(s)}
  + \underbrace{\sum_{\substack{n=1\\n\equiv 2(3)}}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}}_{L_2(s)}
\end{equation}
The fixed-point sector ($n \equiv 0$) contributes exactly $3^{-s}\zeta(s)$;
the twist sectors ($n \equiv \pm 1$) together $(1 - 3^{-s})\zeta(s)$.

\subsection{Principal character $\chi_0$ mod~3}

The Dirichlet character $\chi_0 \bmod 3$ with $\chi_0(n) = 0$ if $3 \mid n$,
otherwise $\chi_0(n) = 1$, gives \BM:
\begin{equation}
  L(s,\chi_0) = \sum_{\gcd(n,3)=1} \frac{1}{n^s}
  = (1 - 3^{-s})\,\zeta(s)
  = \prod_{p \neq 3} \frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}
\end{equation}
This is the \textbf{spectral zeta function of the T$^4$/Z$_3$ torus}: the
characteristic prime $p=3$ does not appear as an Euler factor — it is the
geometric foundation of the orbifold and stands outside the prime hierarchy.

\subsection{Non-trivial character $\chi_1$ mod~3}

The character $\chi_1 \bmod 3$ with $\chi_1(1) = +1$, $\chi_1(2) = -1$,
$\chi_1(0) = 0$ measures the \emph{asymmetry} of the twist sectors \BM:
\begin{equation}
  L(s,\chi_1) = \sum_{\substack{n=1\\n\equiv 1(3)}}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}
              - \sum_{\substack{n=1\\n\equiv 2(3)}}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}
\end{equation}
with the exact value (Dirichlet 1837): $L(1,\chi_1) = \pi/(3\sqrt{3})$.

Physical meaning \SM: $L(s,\chi_1)$ measures the difference between winding
numbers $w = +1$ and $w = -1$ on the torus, i.e.\ the Z$_3$ sector asymmetry.
Whether $L(s,\chi_1)$ has a direct physical observable in FFGFT
(particle–antiparticle asymmetry in the twisted sector?) remains open \SM.

\section{Theorem B: Hierarchical Euler product by $k = \mathrm{ord}_p(3)$}
% ============================================================

The primes in $L(s,\chi_0) = \prod_{p\neq 3}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ are not
equivalent. By the entry-depth rule $k = \mathrm{ord}_p(3)$ from
Doc.~342, Theorem~B, the Euler product is hierarchically ordered \BM:
\begin{equation}
  L(s,\chi_0) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty} \prod_{\substack{p\neq 3\\ \mathrm{ord}_p(3)=k}}
  \frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fractal suppression of higher Euler factors}

On the fractal T$^4$ torus (Doc.~159) high-mode contributions are suppressed
by the damping factor $\tau^{-(1+\xi)k}$. This transfers to the Euler factors;
the \emph{weighted} Euler product reads
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_{T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3}^{\,\mathrm{frac}}(s)
  \;\cong\; \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\;
  \prod_{\substack{p\neq 3\\ \mathrm{ord}_p(3)=k}}
  \left(\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}\right)^{\!\tau^{-k(1+\xi)}}
\end{equation}
Since $\tau^{-k(1+\xi)} \to 0$ as $k\to\infty$, each Euler factor is pushed
toward~1 for large~$k$: primes with $\mathrm{ord}_p(3) \geq 7$ make no physical
contribution \BM. This is Theorem~D of Doc.~342 in the language of the zeta function.

\section{Theorem C: New zeros from the orbifold projection}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Zeros of $(1-3^{-s})$}

The factor $(1-3^{-s})$ in $L(s,\chi_0) = (1-3^{-s})\zeta(s)$
has zeros at \BM:
\begin{equation}
  3^{-s} = 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s = \frac{2\pi i\,k}{\ln 3},
  \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}
\end{equation}
These lie on $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 0$ (imaginary axis), \emph{not} on the
critical line $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 1/2$ of the Riemann hypothesis.
They are absent from the Riemann zeta spectrum; they arise solely from the
Z$_3$ orbifold projection.

The zero spacing $2\pi/\ln 3 \approx 5.72$ encodes the periodicity of the
Z$_3$ lattice structure. A resonance test of the first eight known Riemann
zeros $\gamma_n$ against this unit shows no significant coincidence
(deviations $0.15$--$0.47$) \BM: the full Riemann zeta encodes the distribution
of \emph{all} primes, not only the Z$_3$-compatible ones.

\subsection{Complete zero structure of the orbifold zeta}

$L(s,\chi_0)$ has three types of zeros:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$}: at $s = -2,-4,-6,\ldots$
  \item \textbf{Non-trivial Riemann zeros}: on $\mathrm{Re}(s)=1/2$ (assumed), unchanged
  \item \textbf{Orbifold zeros}: at $s = 2\pi ik/\ln 3$, $k\neq 0$
        — new, generated by the Z$_3$ projection \BM
\end{itemize}

\section{Theorem D: Four Hecke L-functions of the GF$(27)$ structure}

\subsection{Galois group and its characters}

The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$
has three characters: the trivial $\chi_\mathrm{triv}$ and two non-trivial
$\chi_\omega$, $\chi_{\omega^2}$ with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ \BM.

The four Frobenius conjugacy classes of primitive elements in GF$(27)^*$
(Doc.~342, Theorem~A: polynomials $f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4$) correspond to four
Hecke L-functions describing the splitting behaviour of primes in the extension

\subsection{Open question}

Theorem~A of Doc.~342 assigns the four primitive cubics $f_1,\ldots,f_4$ to
the four Frobenius conjugacy classes in GF$(27)^*$.
Whether these four classes carry physically distinct mass layers
(analogous to $f_1$ carrying the lepton layer) is an open question \SM.
If so, each layer would be characterised by its own Hecke L-function.
