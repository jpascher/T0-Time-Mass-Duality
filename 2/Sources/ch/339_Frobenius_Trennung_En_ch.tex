\begin{abstract}
The multiplicative group $GF(27)^*\cong\mathbb{Z}_{26}$ decomposes under the
Frobenius automorphism $\phi:x\mapsto x^3$ into exactly two types: two fixed
points $\{+1,-1\}$ and eight three-element orbits. Via the decomposition
$\mathbb{Z}_{26}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_{13}$ (CRT), four
$\mathbb{Z}_{13}$ three-orbits times two $\mathbb{Z}_2$ parities yield the
eight gluons of the adjoint $SU(3)$ representation; the fixed field $GF(3)$
of the Frobenius supplies the $U(1)$ sector (photon). The fixed points
$\{+1,-1\}$ correspond to the massive $\mathbb{Z}_2$ sector
(particle/antiparticle). All results are algebraically forced. Status: \KM.
\end{abstract}

$\tilde{T}\cdot m=1$ has no solution at $m=0$ — FFGFT knows no empty

m_0 = \frac{E_H}{c^2} \approx 1{.}4\times10^{-33}\,\text{eV}/c^2.

Gluons and photons are modes in the limit $m\to m_0$, not at $m=0$.
The time circle $S^1_m$ opens to the time axis $\mathbb{R}_t$ in this
limit (type-III pullback, Doc.~332, R96).

The deviation of the dispersion relation from the massless formula

The experimental upper bound on the photon rest mass is
$m_\gamma < 10^{-18}$~eV/$c^2$ (PDG). The FFGFT minimum mass $m_0$
lies $10^{15}$ times below this — beyond any conceivable measurement.

``Massless'' in FFGFT is not an algebraic exactness ($m\neq0$) but a
structural statement: the rest mass lies below the Hubble energy scale
$E_H/c^2$ and is indistinguishable from zero for all physical purposes.
Both poles of the duality are finite and quantified — there is no
infinity, but both limits lie far beyond any experimental reach.

\section{Spin Assignment from the Frobenius Structure \KM}

\subsection{Derivation}

The derivation proceeds in four steps.

\textbf{Step 1 [K]:} The Frobenius map $\phi:x\mapsto x^3$ on $GF(27)^*$
corresponds in the type-III pullback picture (Doc.~332, R96) to the conformal
scaling of the wave vector:
\[
k \;\mapsto\; 3k, \qquad \omega(3k) = 3\,\omega(k).
\]
This follows directly from the massless dispersion relation $\omega=c|k|$.
Massive modes do not satisfy this relation: for $m>0$,
$\omega^2=c^2k^2+(mc^2/\hbar)^2$, so $\omega(3k)\neq3\,\omega(k)$.
Fixed points transform trivially: $\phi(x)=x$ corresponds to $k\mapsto k$.

\textbf{Step 2 [B, standard result]:} In the representation theory of the
Poincaré group (Wigner 1939), massless fields are classified by their behaviour
under scalings $k\mapsto\lambda k$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Spin-1 fields} (vector bosons $A_\mu$): scale linearly,
  $A_\mu\to\lambda\,A_\mu$, i.e.\ $\omega(\lambda k)=\lambda\,\omega(k)$.
\item \emph{Massive fields} (rest mass): invariant in the non-relativistic
  limit ($k\to0$, $\omega\to mc^2/\hbar$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Step 3 [K]:} The 8 three-orbits of $GF(27)^*$ carry the conformal
scaling $k\mapsto3k$ and therefore correspond to massless spin-1 fields.
The 2 fixed points $\{+1,-1\}$ transform trivially and correspond to the
massive sector.

\textbf{Step 4 [K]:} The fixed field $GF(3)^*=\{1,2\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$
is conformally invariant (fixed under the projection $GF(27)\to GF(3)$) and
carries a single spin-1 singlet --- the photon.

\subsection{Compatibility with the FFGFT corpus}

In FFGFT, spin arises from winding numbers $(n_\theta,n_\phi)$ on the spin
bundle $L^2(T^3,S)$ (Doc.~231), not from an external Poincaré representation.
The Wigner classification in Step~2 is therefore a standard result [B] that is
consistent with the FFGFT spin-bundle framework but does not replace it. The
explicit connection --- that the three helicity states of a spin-1 field on
$T^3$ under $\mathbb{Z}_3$ reproduce exactly the three-orbits of $GF(27)^*$
