\section*{Abstract}

Douglas Matzke's hyperbit framework (IPI 2026) derives a stability
threshold
\begin{equation}
  \nthr = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}\,\ln 2} = 6.4097~\text{bits}
\end{equation}
and reads it as a universal number: $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ carries $6.000$~bits
and thus sits $0.41$~bits below the collapse threshold -- matter is stable
because the smallest fermion algebra just barely fails to collapse.
Doc.~326 raised the suspicion that this number hangs on the choice of a
particular temperature. This document tests that numerically and settles it.

\begin{tcolorbox}[ffgftbox, title={Main result \B}]
  With the system-dependent bit energy of FFGFT
  $\Ebit(L) = \hbar c/L$ (Doc.~257) one obtains exactly
  \begin{equation}
    \nthr(L) = \frac{L}{\sqrt{2}\,\lP}
    \label{eq:main}
  \end{equation}
  -- \textbf{linear in the scale}, not an invariant number.
  Matzke's $6.41$ corresponds exactly to the scale
  \begin{equation}
    L_{\mathrm{Matzke}} = \frac{2\pi\,\lP}{\ln 2} = 9.0647\,\lP .
  \end{equation}
  The \enquote{universal} bit value \emph{is} the FFGFT bit energy,
  evaluated at about nine Planck lengths.
  The universality is a concealed choice of scale.
\end{tcolorbox}

What remains \emph{untouched} is the robust core of Matzke's argument:
the collapse condition Compton~$=$~Schwarzschild yields
$\Mcoll = \mP/\sqrt{2}$ purely geometrically -- without any reference to
bits, Landauer, or a temperature. That part stands independently \B.

Verification script: \texttt{326\_nthresh\_skalenabhaengigkeit.py} --
12 assertions, all passed.

\section{The question}

Doc.~326 works out the central divergence between Matzke's framework and
FFGFT: universal vs.\ system-dependent bit values.
The suspicion formulated there was that Matzke's stability threshold
\enquote{hangs on the postulate $T = \TP$} and would itself be
system-dependent under a system-dependent bit energy -- classified as \S.

This question is numerically decidable, and completely so:
one recomputes Matzke's chain, extracts the scale-independent parts,
replaces the bit value by the FFGFT bit energy, and sees whether a
constant remains.

% ============================================================
\section{Matzke's chain}

\subsection{The Landauer bridge}

The temperature used is not the Planck temperature itself but the
\textbf{KMS temperature} of the Planck scale:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{KMS}} = \frac{\TP}{2\pi} = 2.2549\times10^{31}~\mathrm{K}.
\end{equation}
The $2\pi$ comes from the KMS periodicity -- the same one that generates
the Hawking and Unruh temperatures in Doc.~325.
Landauer at this temperature:
\begin{equation}
  \Ebit = k_B T_{\mathrm{KMS}} \ln 2
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mbit = \frac{\mP \ln 2}{2\pi} = 0.1103\,\mP .
\end{equation}
Numerically reproduced (script~[1], ratio $1.000000000$). \B

\subsection{The collapse condition}

Compton wavelength equal to Schwarzschild radius:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\hbar}{Mc} = \frac{2GM}{c^2}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Mcoll = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{2G}} = \frac{\mP}{\sqrt{2}}
         = 1.5390\times10^{-8}~\mathrm{kg}.
\end{equation}

\begin{tcolorbox}[proofbox, title={The scale-independent core \B}]
  $\Mcoll = \mP/\sqrt{2}$ contains \textbf{no bit value}.
  The collapse condition is purely geometric and identical under any
  bit interpretation. Numerical check:
  $\nthr \cdot \mbit = \Mcoll$ to nine digits (script~[2]).
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{The threshold}

\begin{equation}
  \nthr = \frac{\Mcoll}{\mbit}
        = \frac{\mP/\sqrt{2}}{\mP\ln 2/(2\pi)}
        = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}\,\ln 2}
        = 6.4097 .
\end{equation}

The number is thus a \emph{quotient} of a geometric numerator and a
posited denominator. Everything about it that is not geometric sits in
the bit value.

% ============================================================
\section{The same computation with system-dependent bit energy}

Instead of a universal bit value, FFGFT posits (Doc.~257, A271--A273):
\begin{equation}
  \Ebit(L) = \frac{\hbar c}{L}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mbit(L) = \frac{\hbar}{cL}.
\end{equation}
Inserted into $\nthr = \Mcoll/\mbit$:
\begin{equation}
  \nthr(L) = \frac{\mP}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{cL}{\hbar}
           = \frac{L}{\sqrt{2}\,\lP},
\end{equation}
since $\lP = \hbar/(c\,\mP)$. Numerically confirmed for
$L \in \{1, 5, 10\}\,\lP$ to machine precision (script~[3]). \B

\begin{tcolorbox}[warnbox, title={What follows}]
  $\nthr$ is a \textbf{linear function of the scale}, not a constant.
  It has no distinguished value as long as no scale is distinguished.
  The question \enquote{how many bits until collapse?} is unanswerable
  without specifying the scale -- it is underdetermined.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Matzke's implicit scale}

Solving $\mbit = \hbar/(cL)$ for $L$ and inserting Matzke's bit value:
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{Matzke}} = \frac{\hbar}{c\,\mbit}
                      = \frac{2\pi\,\lP}{\ln 2}
                      = 9.0647\,\lP
                      = 1.4651\times10^{-34}~\mathrm{m}.
\end{equation}
And indeed Eq.~\eqref{eq:main} returns $\nthr = 6.4097$ at this scale --
exactly Matzke's value (script~[4]). \B

\begin{tcolorbox}[ffgftbox, title={Core finding}]
  Matzke's universal bit value is the FFGFT bit energy
  $\Ebit = \hbar c/L$, evaluated at $L = 2\pi\lP/\ln 2$.
  Both frameworks compute with the same quantity; the difference is that
  FFGFT carries the scale along as a free parameter, whereas Matzke fixes
  one particular scale and calls the result universal.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{\enquote{It from bit} -- with resolution depth}

The finding of this document reads as criticism. It is, however, above all
a \emph{refinement}, and read that way it confirms Matzke's underlying
intuition rather than refuting it.

\subsection{The analogy to digital technology}

In computing, a bit has no absolute meaning.
\enquote{16 bits} says nothing until it is clear: 16 bits \emph{of what}?
A 16-bit colour value, a 16-bit audio sample and a 16-bit address space
carry the same bit count and denote entirely different physical
quantities. What a bit \emph{means} is fixed only by the quantisation --
the spacing between two distinguishable states. For audio that is the
amplitude resolution, for images the colour depth, for instruments the
size of the least significant bit.

\begin{tcolorbox}[ffgftbox, title={$\Ebit = \hbar c/L$ is a resolution depth}]
  The FFGFT bit energy does not say \enquote{a bit weighs this much} but
  \enquote{at this scale a distinction costs this much}.
  $\hbar c$ is the exchange rate between distinction and energy;
  $L$ states at which level the distinguishing happens.
  A bit at nuclear scale is a different physical object from a bit at
  atomic scale -- although both carry exactly one yes/no decision.
\end{tcolorbox}

Wheeler's \emph{it from bit} is thereby not refuted but sharpened:
yes, physics can be built from distinguishability -- but information is
nothing absolute, it is always relative to a resolution. The missing
parameter in the classical formulation is the resolution depth.

In this language Matzke's choice of a fixed bit value is not wrong but a
fixing of the \emph{word width}: one fixes the quantisation and computes --
a familiar and legitimate procedure from engineering practice. The only
fallacy is to mistake the chosen word width for a constant of nature.
What Matzke actually demonstrated -- that the whole of black-hole physics
can be formulated as pure bit bookkeeping -- stands untouched and is the
real result.

\subsection{The floor of resolution}

Unlike in digital technology, the resolution depth in FFGFT is bounded
from below:
\begin{equation}
  L \geq L_0 = \xi\cdot\lP = 2.155\times10^{-39}~\mathrm{m}
  \qquad \text{(Doc.~180 \K)}.
\end{equation}
$L_0$ is the counterpart of the smallest representable quantisation step.
Below it there are no distinctions left -- not because one could not
measure precisely enough, but because there is nothing left to
distinguish. The maximal resolution depth is thus finite, and precisely
from this it follows in Doc.~327 that the spectral operator $\hat F$ is
bounded and self-adjoint. There are no infinities in FFGFT.
On why $\lP$ as a reference scale is not arbitrary -- and where the
justification of the factor $\xi$ nevertheless remains open -- see the
following subsection.

\subsection{Why $\lP$ is not an arbitrary scale}

Here an obvious objection arises: if all scales are on an equal footing
and only the resolution depth changes, why should there be a floor at
all -- and why precisely at $\xi$ times the Planck length? Would that not
be the very fallacy charged against Matzke: distinguishing a scale
without justifying it?

The answer is in Section~3 of this document. $\lP$ is not one scale among
many but precisely the one at which Compton wavelength and Schwarzschild
radius coincide -- that is, the collapse condition, which was shown there
to be \emph{purely geometric and free of any bit value}
($\Mcoll = \mP/\sqrt{2}$, with no reference to bits, Landauer, or
temperature). The situation is therefore asymmetric:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.2cm}p{8.3cm}@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Matzke's} $L = 2\pi\lP/\ln 2$ & \textbf{FFGFT's} $\lP$ \\
  \midrule
  comes from a Landauer evaluation at a posited temperature &
  is the scale at which geometry itself tips over: below it no
  distinguishable object exists, because it collapses \\[3pt]
  has no independent justification -- \emph{chosen} &
  follows from $\xi$ via $G = \xi^2/(4m_e)$ -- \emph{derived} \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

What matters is the direction of the derivation chain (Doc.~180):
\begin{equation}
  \xi = \lambda_{\min}(\hat F_{D_4})
  \;\Longrightarrow\; G = \frac{\xi^2}{4m_e}
  \;\Longrightarrow\; \lP = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\; L_0 = \xi\cdot\lP .
\end{equation}
$\xi$ is a spectral value, determined independently of any length
(Docs.~322, 327). In FFGFT $\lP$ is a \emph{derived} quantity, not an
input. The chain runs from the algebra to the length, not the reverse.

\begin{tcolorbox}[proofbox, title={The floor follows from the Lagrangian \B\ (R89/R90)}]
  The last step of the chain is better founded than Doc.~180 presents it.
  There $L_0 = \xi^1\cdot\lP$ is justified by the coupling term
  $g_T^\ell = \xi\, m_\ell$ carrying one power of $\xi$ -- an argument by
  analogy. The exponent, however, follows \emph{necessarily} from the mass
  term: the time field $\Delta m$ is a massive mediator with
  \begin{equation}
    m_T = \frac{M_{\mathrm{base}}}{\xi}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \text{range} = \frac{\hbar}{m_T c}
    = \xi\cdot\lambda_C(M_{\mathrm{base}}),
  \end{equation}
  hence range $\sim\xi^1$, because $m_T\sim\xi^{-1}$ and
  $\lambda_C\sim 1/m$. In the fundamental sector ($M_{\mathrm{base}} = \mP$)
  the range is exactly $\xi\lP = L_0$ \B.

  This also answers \emph{why} a floor exists at all: because the mediator
  is massive. Below its Compton range the time field can no longer
  mediate -- there \enquote{distance} loses its meaning. No postulate, but
  propagator physics.

  The same rule explains the apparently contradictory $m_T$ values in the
  corpus: Doc.~032 evaluates it in the lepton sector
  ($M_{\mathrm{base}} = m_e$, $m_T \approx 5.22$~GeV) and itself calls the
  result an \emph{effective} torsion mass. Universal form,
  sector-dependent value -- structurally identical with $\Ebit = \hbar c/L$.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[warnbox, title={What remains open \S}]
  The choice $M_{\mathrm{base}} = \mP$ for the fundamental sector
  introduces no new parameter ($\lambda = 1$ in Planck units) but is not
  independently justified (R90).

  The Schwarzschild check mentioned in Doc.~190 (Clarification~4) offers
  no support: an object with $M_0 = \xi\mP/2$ has Schwarzschild radius
  $\xi\lP$, but this is an \emph{identity} -- from $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ and
  $\lP = G\mP/c^2$ it follows for any value of $\xi$ and any exponent
  (R92). It tests nothing.
\end{tcolorbox}

For the arguments of this document and for Doc.~327 this has no
consequences: both require only that \emph{some} finite floor exists, not
its precise value. The number of rungs of the scale ladder is finite for
any $L_0 = \xi^n\lP$ with finite $n$, so $\sum_n r_n < \infty$ and hence
the boundedness of $\hat F$ hold independently of the exponent.
What remains open is the \emph{location} of the floor, not its existence.

The difference from Matzke's choice of scale thus persists and is sharper
than the preceding analysis suggested: with Matzke the scale is chosen,
and the choice carries the result. With FFGFT the reference scale $\lP$ is
derived from $\xi$ and the factor below it follows from the mediator mass;
what remains open is only why the base mass of the fundamental sector is
$\mP$.

\subsection{The rules are scale-invariant, the values are not}

The decisive point: that the \emph{values} are scale-dependent does not
mean the \emph{rules} are. In FFGFT the same structure holds at every
scale:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Form} & \textbf{scale-invariant} \\
  \midrule
  Time-mass duality & $\tilde T\cdot m = 1$ & yes \\
  Bit energy & $\Ebit = \hbar c/L$ & yes (form), no (value) \\
  KMS read-off rule & $T = \hbar/(k_B\tau)$ & yes \\
  Information unit & $\Delta w = 1$ (winding quantum) & yes \\
  $\mathbb{Z}_3$ triality selection & $T_R = 0$ & yes \\
  Fractal dimension & $D_f = 3-\xi$ & yes \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The KMS rule, for instance, tempers Unruh, Gibbons--Hawking and black
holes with \emph{one} formula across 22 orders of magnitude in $\tau$
(Docs.~313, 325). The triality selection $T_R = 0$ acts in hadron
confinement just as it does at the horizon of a black hole
(Docs.~321, 325). The winding quantum $\Delta w = 1$ is the same
topological quantity at every scale -- only its energy is scale-specific.

\begin{tcolorbox}[proofbox, title={The actual finding}]
  Scale-invariant \emph{rules} with scale-dependent \emph{values} are no
  contradiction but the normal case in any field theory -- consider
  running couplings: $\alpha_s(\mu)$ changes its value with the scale,
  while the Callan--Symanzik equation describing that running holds
  scale-independently.\\[4pt]
  $\Ebit(L) = \hbar c/L$ behaves in exactly this way: the \emph{formula}
  is universal, the \emph{value} is not. Whoever takes the value to be
  universal mistakes the rule for one of its evaluations.
\end{tcolorbox}

With this, \enquote{it from bit} can be stated precisely in FFGFT:
the world is built from distinctions, and the rules by which distinctions
compose into physics are the same at all scales. What changes with the
scale is only the price of a distinction -- the resolution depth.
Matzke's $6.41$ is then no error but the right number for the word width
$L = 2\pi\lP/\ln 2$.

\section{Placement}

\subsection{What falls}

The reading of $\nthr = 6.41$ as a \emph{fundamental number} of the
universe. It is the quotient of a geometric quantity and a posited bit
value; the postulate is a choice of scale, and across scales the result
varies by 29 orders of magnitude. The statement
\enquote{$\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ lies $0.41$~bits below the threshold} holds
only at $L = 9.06\,\lP$ and does not survive beyond it. \X

\subsection{What stands}

Considerably more than the criticism might suggest:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Mcoll = \mP/\sqrt{2}$ from Compton~$=$~Schwarzschild -- purely
    geometric, independent of any bit interpretation \B.
  \item The Schwarzschild radius without field equations: Matzke's
    Novel Result \#1 is untouched by this analysis \B.
  \item $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ as a fermion generation (Furey), the three
    falling out, the algebraic structure as a whole \B.
  \item The Hawking mechanism as an algebraic picture -- the structural
    correspondences to FFGFT are exact (Doc.~326).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Consequence for FFGFT}

The analysis is not merely a refutation; it supports the FFGFT position
of Doc.~257 and A271--A273 from the outside.
If a framework that explicitly posits a universal bit value can be
reconstructed as the evaluation of the scale-dependent formula
$\Ebit = \hbar c/L$ at one particular scale, then scale dependence is
not the special case but the general form.

This fits with Doc.~325: there the Hawking temperature is derivable
without a universal bit value ($k_B T_H = \Ebit(4\pi r_s)$ exactly), and
the numerical check in Doc.~326 gave
$T_H(\text{Matzke})/T_H(\text{KMS}) = 1.000000$.
Both findings say the same thing: the universal bit value is a
reparametrization, not an independent physical input.

Worth noting is the origin of the $2\pi$ in Matzke's bit value:
it comes from the KMS periodicity, i.e.\ from exactly the relation that
generates the Hawking temperature in Doc.~325 \emph{without} a bit value.
