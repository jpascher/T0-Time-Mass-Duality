\section*{Abstract}

The mode functions $\phi_{n,l,j}$ from Doc.~202 (Part II) hold
for a simple torus with $D_f = 3$. The fractal dimension
$D_f = 3-\xi_0$ of the FFGFT torus leads to a correction of first
order in $\xi_0$. This correction modifies the normalisation and the
dispersion relation, but leaves the basic structure of the wave functions
unchanged. The masses still arise as eigenvalue residues
after almost complete compensation.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Starting point: simple torus (\texorpdfstring{$D_f = 3$}{Df = 3})}
%------------------------------------------------------------

On the simple torus $T^3$ with side length $L_0$ the
standard integration measure applies:
\begin{equation}
  d\mu_0 = d^3x, \qquad
  \int_{T^3} d\mu_0 = L_0^3 = V
\end{equation}

The mode functions (Doc.~202):
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{n,l,j}^{(0)}(t,\mathbf{x})
  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\,
    e^{i\mathbf{k}_{n,l}\cdot\mathbf{x}} \cdot
    Y_{l,j}(\theta,\varphi) \cdot
    e^{-im_{n,l,j}t}
  \label{eq:phi0}
\end{equation}

are exactly normalised: $\int_{T^3}|\phi^{(0)}|^2\,d\mu_0 = 1$.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The fractal measure}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Definition}

On the FFGFT torus with fractal dimension $D_f = 3-\xi_0$ the
integration measure is modified. For a fractal medium with
Hausdorff dimension $D_f$ the scaled measure applies:

\begin{equation}
  d\mu_{D_f} = \left(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)^{D_f - 3} d^3x
  = \left(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)^{-\xi_0} d^3x
  \label{eq:fraktales-mass}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Expansion for small \texorpdfstring{$\xi_0$}{xi0}}

Since $\xi_0 = 4/30000 \ll 1$, we expand:
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)^{-\xi_0}
  = 1 - \xi_0 \ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0} + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \label{eq:entwicklung}
\end{equation}

The fractal measure is therefore:
\begin{equation}
  d\mu_{D_f} = \left(1 - \xi_0\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)d^3x
  + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \label{eq:mass-entwickelt}
\end{equation}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Corrected normalisation to first order}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The normalisation integral}

The corrected normalisation condition reads:
\begin{equation}
  \int_{T^3}|\phi_{n,l,j}|^2\,d\mu_{D_f} = 1
\end{equation}

With the ansatz $\phi_{n,l,j} = N_{n,l,j} \cdot \phi^{(0)}_{n,l,j}$:
\begin{align}
  N_{n,l,j}^{-2}
  &= \int_{T^3}|\phi^{(0)}|^2\,d\mu_{D_f} \nonumber \\
  &= \int_{T^3}\frac{1}{V}\left(1 - \xi_0\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)d^3x
  \label{eq:norm-integral}
\end{align}

\subsection{Calculation of the correction term}

The integral of the correction term:
\begin{equation}
  I_{\text{frak}} = -\frac{\xi_0}{V}
  \int_{T^3}\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\,d^3x
  \label{eq:I-frak}
\end{equation}

On the cubic torus $[0,L_0]^3$, $|\mathbf{x}|$ is
the distance from the origin. The integral yields:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{V}\int_{T^3}\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\,d^3x
  = \ln\langle r\rangle/L_0
  \approx \ln(c_0)
\end{equation}

with a geometric constant $c_0 \approx 0.5$ (mean
relative distance in the torus). Hence:
\begin{equation}
  N_{n,l,j}^{-2} = 1 + \xi_0|\ln c_0| + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \approx 1 + 0.69\,\xi_0
  \label{eq:N-quadrat}
\end{equation}
\textit{(Note of 30~Sept.~2026: In general $\langle\ln r\rangle \neq \ln\langle r\rangle$. Numerically (Monte Carlo): for $[0,L_0]^3$ with distance from the origin $\langle r\rangle = 0.961\,L_0$, $\langle\ln(r/L_0)\rangle = -0.094$; for the torus distance (centred) $\langle r\rangle = 0.480\,L_0$, $\langle\ln(r/L_0)\rangle = -0.787$. The coefficient $0.69 = |\ln 0.5|$ follows in no reading; one would obtain $N = 1 - 6.3 \cdot 10^{-6}$ or $1 - 5.2 \cdot 10^{-5}$ instead of $1 - 4.6 \cdot 10^{-5}$.)}

\subsection{Corrected normalisation factor}

Up to first order in $\xi_0$:
\begin{equation}
  N_{n,l,j} = 1 - \frac{0.69}{2}\,\xi_0 + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \approx 1 - 4.6 \times 10^{-5}
  \label{eq:N-wert}
\end{equation}

The correction is tiny ($\sim 10^{-5}$), consistent with
the fact that $\xi_0 \ll 1$.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Corrected dispersion relation}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The fractal Laplace operator}

On the fractal torus the Laplace operator is modified.
For small $\xi_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{D_f} = \Delta_3 + \xi_0 \cdot \delta\Delta + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \label{eq:frak-laplace}
\end{equation}

The correction operator $\delta\Delta$ arises from the
modification of the measure:
\begin{equation}
  \delta\Delta\,\phi
  = -\nabla\!\left(\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\right)\cdot\nabla\phi
  = -\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|^2}\cdot\nabla\phi
  \label{eq:delta-Delta}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Eigenvalue correction}

The corrected Klein--Gordon equation:
\begin{equation}
  \left[\Box + \xi_0\cdot\delta\Box\right]\phi_{n,l,j}
  = -m_{n,l,j}^2\,\phi_{n,l,j}
\end{equation}

For the plane wave $e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}$,
$\delta\Delta$ yields to first order:
\begin{equation}
  \langle\phi^{(0)}|\delta\Delta|\phi^{(0)}\rangle
  = -\langle\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|^2}\cdot\nabla\rangle
  = -i\,k\langle\cos\theta_{kx}/r\rangle
\end{equation}

On the torus this term averages to zero, since
$\langle \mathbf{x}/|\mathbf{x}|^2 \rangle = 0$ (symmetry).

The eigenfrequency correction comes from the scalar part:
\begin{equation}
  \delta\omega_{n,l,j}^2
  = -\xi_0 \cdot k_{n,l}^2 \cdot \langle\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{L_0}\rangle
  \approx 0.69\,\xi_0 \cdot k_{n,l}^2
  \label{eq:omega-korrektur}
\end{equation}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The corrected mode functions}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The complete mode functions up to first order in $\xi_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \phi_{n,l,j}^{(D_f)}(t,\mathbf{x})
    = \frac{N_{n,l,j}}{\sqrt{V}}\,
      e^{i\mathbf{k}_{n,l}\cdot\mathbf{x}} \cdot
      Y_{l,j}(\theta,\varphi) \cdot
      e^{-im_{n,l,j}^{(D_f)}\,t}
  }
  \label{eq:phi-korrigiert}
\end{equation}

with:
\begin{align}
  N_{n,l,j} &= 1 - 4.6\times10^{-5} + \mathcal{O}(\xi_0^2)
  \label{eq:N-final} \\
  \left(m_{n,l,j}^{(D_f)}\right)^2
  &= k_{n,l}^2\left(1 + 0.69\,\xi_0\right) + \langle\hat{V}\rangle
  \label{eq:m-korrigiert}
\end{align}

The fractal correction $0.69\,\xi_0 \approx 9.2 \times 10^{-5}$ \textit{(Corrected on 30~Sept.~2026: previously $3 \times 10^{-5}$ -- $0.69 \cdot 1.3333 \cdot 10^{-4} = 9.2 \cdot 10^{-5}$)}
to the kinetic energy does not change the origin of mass qualitatively:
the masses remain eigenvalue residues after almost complete
compensation; the correction appears only in the fifth decimal place.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Physical significance}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Why the correction remains small}

The fractal correction is of order $\xi_0 \approx 1.3\times10^{-4}$.
This is consistent with the mass hierarchy: the particle masses are
of order $\xi_0^{p_i} \cdot v$, i.e.\ themselves suppressed by powers
of $\xi_0$. The wave-function correction lies at the same order.

\subsection{Orthogonality is preserved}

Since the correction \eqref{eq:N-final} is mode-independent
(the same $N$ for all $(n,l,j)$), orthogonality is preserved:
\begin{equation}
  \int_{T^3}
  \phi_{n,l,j}^{(D_f)*}\,\phi_{n',l',j'}^{(D_f)}\,d\mu_{D_f}
  = \delta_{nn'}\delta_{ll'}\delta_{jj'}
\end{equation}

The non-closure theorem (Doc.~193) holds unchanged.

\subsection{Completeness}

The picture of mass generation from Doc.~202 (Part V) remains correct:
\[
  m_i^2 = (2\pi n/L_0)^2\cdot(1+0.69\,\xi_0) + \langle\hat{V}\rangle
  \approx (2\pi n/L_0)^2 + \langle\hat{V}\rangle
\]

The correction $(1+0.69\,\xi_0)$ is numerically irrelevant compared
with the compensation over 32--39 orders of magnitude.

\section*{Summary}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The fractal boundary condition arising from $D_f = 3-\xi_0$ modifies the
mode functions of Doc.~202 to first order:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Normalisation factor: $N = 1 - 4.6\times10^{-5}$
  \item Dispersion correction: $+0.69\,\xi_0$ to the kinetic energy
\end{itemize}
Both corrections are of order $\xi_0 \approx 1.3\times10^{-4}$
and do not change the physical structure. The task from Doc.~202,
Problem~2, is thereby formally completed.
