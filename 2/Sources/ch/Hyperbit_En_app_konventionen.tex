\section*{Layer Markings}

Every substantive statement in this book carries a layer marking.
This convention originates from the FFGFT corpus (Doc.\,A010):

\begin{description}
  \item[\status{SETZUNG}] Declared starting point. Axiom, stipulation, not provable
    within the system --- but explicitly named as such.
  \item[\status{B}] Algebraically/mathematically derived from declared foundations.
    Formal derivation step.
  \item[\QQ{}] Source. External primary source or measured value. Can be verified
    with the corpus.
  \item[\status{S}] Plausibility sketch. Argument that convinces, but is not
    formally proven.
  \item[\status{H}] Open research question. Explicitly not answered.
  \item[\status{K}] Core result. Summary that is stand-alone readable even without
    the proof.
\end{description}

\section*{Mathematical Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\xi_{\text{par}} = 4/30000$ --- FFGFT fundamental parameter
  \item $\lP = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}$ --- Planck length
  \item $\mP = \sqrt{\hbar c/G}$ --- Planck mass
  \item $E_P = \mP c^2$ --- Planck energy
  \item $T_P = E_P / k_B$ --- Planck temperature
  \item $\Ebit = \hbar c / L$ --- Bit energy on scale $L$ (FFGFT)
  \item $\mbit = \mP \ln 2 / (2\pi)$ --- Bit mass (Matzke)
  \item $W$ --- Phase space volume in units of $h^f$
  \item $f$ --- Dimension of the phase space
\end{itemize}
