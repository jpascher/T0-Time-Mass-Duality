\section*{Layer Markers}

Every substantive statement in this book carries a layer marker.
This convention comes from the FFGFT corpus (Dok.\,A010):

\begin{description}
  \item[\status{SETZUNG}] Declared starting point. Axiom, postulate, not provable
    within the system --- but explicitly named as such.
  \item[\status{B}] Algebraically/mathematically derived from declared foundations.
    Formal derivation step.
  \item[\QQ{}] Source. External primary source or measured value. Kann mit dem Korpus
    überprüft werden.
  \item[\status{S}] Plausibility sketch. Argument that convinces, but has not been
    formally proved.
  \item[\status{H}] Open research question. Explicitly not answered.
  \item[\status{K}] Key result. Summary that is readable standalone
    even without the proof.
\end{description}

\section*{Mathematical Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\xi_{\text{par}} = 4/30000$ --- FFGFT-Grundparameter
  \item $\lP = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}$ --- Planck-Länge
  \item $\mP = \sqrt{\hbar c/G}$ --- Planck-Masse
  \item $E_P = \mP c^2$ --- Planck-Energie
  \item $T_P = E_P / k_B$ --- Planck-Temperatur
  \item $\Ebit = \hbar c / L$ --- Bit-Energie auf Skala $L$ (FFGFT)
  \item $\mbit = \mP \ln 2 / (2\pi)$ --- Bit-Masse (Matzke)
  \item $W$ --- Phase space volume in units of $h^f$
  \item $f$ --- Dimension of the phase space
\end{itemize}
