\begin{abstract}
The Koide angle $\theta=2/9$ (Doc.~292) and the icosahedral projection
$p_0=|\langle v_0\mid R_5\,v_e\rangle|^2=2/9$ (Doc.~293) arise from two
independent calculations and land on exactly the same rational number.
This document names the reason and draws the complete consequence.

The reason: both quantities are the same quotient $2/3^2$ -- numerator = number
of non-trivial $\mathbb{Z}_3$ modes, denominator = square of the $\mathbb{Z}_3$
order. $\theta$ reads it as the phase of the mass operator, $p_0$ as the modulus
squared of an algebraic projection. This is the Born-rule structure: the same
number, once as a modulus and once as a modulus squared of the same matrix element.

The consequence: the single matrix element $A=\langle v_0\mid R_5\,v_e\rangle$ of
the $A_5$ embedding determines the Koide formula completely and parameter-free.
Its modulus supplies the amplitude coefficient $\sqrt2 = 3|A|$, its modulus
squared supplies the phase $\theta = |A|^2 = 2/9$:
\[
  a_k = 1 + 3\,|A|\cdot\cos\!\bigl(|A|^2 + \tfrac{2\pi k}{3}\bigr),
  \qquad A = \langle v_0\mid R_5\,v_e\rangle.
\]
No free parameter. No external $\theta$. The mass amplitudes of the charged
leptons follow directly from the $A_5$ geometry.

What remains transcendental: $\cos(2/9)$, since $2/9\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}$.
Exact algebraic mass amplitudes are therefore excluded in principle.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Common Quotient $2/3^2$}

\subsection{$p_0$ as a Count in the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Mode Space}

The three lepton modes are the Fourier eigenmodes of the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ circulant on the
$\mathbb{C}^3$ fibre (Docs.~282, 288):
\[
  v_0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(1,1,1),\qquad
  v_{1,2} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(1,\omega^{\pm1},\omega^{\pm2}).
\]
One mode is trivial ($v_0$, symmetric under $\mathbb{Z}_3$), two are non-trivial
($v_1,v_2$, Galois-conjugate). The electron mode is one of the two non-trivial ones (which
one is decided by the sign of the angle, Doc.~293).

When $v_e = v_1$ is rotated by $R_5$, the mode redistributes over all three directions with
the weights (Doc.~293, \BM):
\[
  p_0 = \frac{2}{9},\qquad
  p_2 = \frac{5-3\varphi}{9},\qquad
  p_1 = \frac{2+3\varphi}{9},\qquad
  p_0+p_1+p_2 = 1.
\]
All three denominators are $9 = 3^2$. For $p_0$, the numerator is the small integer~$2$ --
the number of non-trivial modes. In words:
\[
  p_0 = \frac{\#\,\text{non-trivial }\mathbb{Z}_3\text{ modes}}{|\mathbb{Z}_3|^2}
  = \frac{2}{3^2}.
\]
The denominator is the square, not the group order itself, because $p_0$ is the modulus
squared of an amplitude: $|\langle v_0|R_5 v_e\rangle| = \sqrt{2}/3$, and
$(\sqrt2/3)^2 = 2/9$. The factor~$3$ in the amplitude's denominator is the normalisation of
the modes ($1/\sqrt3$ per vector, two vectors in the inner product); the factor $\sqrt2$ in the
numerator comes from the icosahedral geometry (axis angle $37.38^\circ$ between the three-fold
and five-fold axes, Doc.~293).

\subsection{$\theta$ as a Phase in the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Mode Space}

The Koide parametrisation (Doc.~292)
\[
  \sqrt{m_k} = M\,\bigl(1+\sqrt2\cos(\theta + 2\pi k/3)\bigr),\qquad k=0,1,2,
\]
carries two structural numbers: the amplitude $\sqrt2$ and the phase $\theta$. Doc.~353 shows
that $\sqrt2$ follows from $\mathbb{Z}_3$ equidistribution \BM. Doc.~291 shows in which channel
$\theta$ lives: not in the symmetric modulus functional (where $Q = 2/3$ for every $\theta$),
but in the phase channel of the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ holonomy.

The empirical value is $\theta = 2/9$ (Doc.~292: the PDG back-calculated value deviates from
$2/9$ by only $0.9\sigma$, $\tau$-limited; the exact decimal depends on the $m_\tau$ central
value used, the $\sigma$ assessment does not). Read as a fraction, $\theta = 2/3^2$. Here too
the numerator~$2$ stands for the two non-trivial modes and the denominator $3^2$ for the square
of the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ dimension.

Why the square? Because $\theta$ measures the phase on the \emph{product space} of the modes --
not on the group $\mathbb{Z}_3$ (where the natural fraction would be $2/3$, the share of
non-trivial elements), but on $\mathbb{C}^3 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3$, the space of mass-operator
elements. The Hermitian circulant $M_{ij}$ has $3^2 = 9$ entries, and the phase that pulls $M$
away from the symmetry axis is a fraction of that $9$-dimensional space.

\subsection{The Same Asymmetry, Read Twice}

Both quantities count the same thing:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
  \theta = p_0 = \frac{\#\,\text{non-trivial }\mathbb{Z}_3\text{ modes}}{|\mathbb{Z}_3|^2}
  = \frac{2}{3^2} = \frac{2}{9}.
\end{equation}
$p_0$ reads this quotient as a \emph{probability}: how much of the electron mode lands in the
symmetric direction under the icosahedral rotation? $\theta$ reads it as a \emph{phase}: how far
is the mass configuration from full $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry? Both times the asymmetry of the
lepton configuration relative to full $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry is measured -- once as a share,
once as an angle.

\section{The Structure of the Born Rule}

This is the structure of quantum mechanics. The Born rule \EM{} links a complex amplitude
$A = \langle\psi|\phi\rangle$ to a measurable probability:
\[
  P = |A|^2.
\]
The phase of $A$ is not directly measurable; only $|A|^2$ is. Yet the phase carries the
interference information -- it becomes visible as soon as two amplitudes are superposed.

The Koide structure exhibits the same dual character, in a particularly simple form:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The amplitude of the icosahedral projection is $|\langle v_0|R_5 v_e\rangle| = \sqrt2/3$
    (modulus; not an adjustable parameter, Doc.~293).
  \item Its square is the probability $p_0 = 2/9$.
  \item The Koide angle $\theta = 2/9$ is \emph{numerically the same quotient}, read as a phase.
\end{itemize}
The relation is not $\theta = |A|$ (which would be $\sqrt2/3 \approx 0.471$) and not
$\theta = \arg A$ (which would be a rotation angle of the projection), but $\theta = |A|^2 = p_0$.
That is the core: \emph{the phase of the mass configuration equals the probability of the mode
redistribution}, because both realise the quotient~\eqref{eq:quotient}.

That the same number appears in both roles is not a numerical accident but a property of the
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ mode space: the denominator $3^2$ is forced in both roles -- as a normalisation
square in $p_0$, as the dimension of the operator space in $\theta$. The numerator~$2$ is in both
roles the number of non-trivial modes. This is the algebraic form of what Doc.~293 describes as
an \enquote{invariant of translation}: the number belongs to the structure, not to the
representation.

\[
  3\cdot|\langle v_0 \mid R_5\, v_e\rangle| = 3\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{3} = \sqrt2.

\[
  p_0 = |\langle v_0 \mid R_5\, v_e\rangle|^2 = \frac{2}{9}.

Since this square enters the cosine directly as the phase ($\theta = p_0 = 2/9$
rad), the Born rule appears as a geometric coupling: the amplitude and the
