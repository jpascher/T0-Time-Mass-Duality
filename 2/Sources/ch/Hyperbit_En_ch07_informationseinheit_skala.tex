\section{The Question}

Where does the number $k_BT\ln 2$ in Landauer's formula come from? Why
exactly this combination of temperature, Boltzmann constant and the logarithm
of 2? And does it apply everywhere --- or only under certain conditions?

The FFGFT gives a precise answer: It applies exactly when the
geometric energy of a winding quantum on the considered scale
coincides with the thermal energy of the bath. That is a
condition, not a universal constant.

The complete derivation follows in three levels. \status{B Doc.\,257}

\section{Level 1: The Winding Quantum --- Topological, Universal}

\begin{keyresult}[Minimal Information Unit --- \status{SETZUNG}]
The minimal information unit in the FFGFT is the \textbf{winding quantum}
$\Delta w = 1$ on the fundamental torus $T^4$.

The homotopy group of the torus is $\pi_1(T^4) \cong \mathbb{Z}^4$ ---
it knows only integer winding numbers. A halved winding quantum
does not exist. This discreteness is a topological fact,
not a postulate. \status{Q}
\end{keyresult}

\textit{For external support:} $\pi_1(T^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ is a
standard result of algebraic topology. It follows from the theorem that
the $n$-dimensional torus $T^n = S^1 \times \cdots \times S^1$ ($n$-fold
product of circles) is, and that $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (the
circle has winding number $\in \mathbb{Z}$), and that the fundamental group
of a product space is the product of the fundamental groups:
\[
  \pi_1(T^4) = \pi_1(S^1)^4 \cong \mathbb{Z}^4.
\]
Textbook references: Hatcher, \textit{Algebraic Topology} \textbf{[T1]},
Ch.~1.2 (freely available: \url{https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf});
Bredon, \textit{Topology and Geometry} \textbf{[T2]}, Ch.~III.§5.
The FFGFT-specific argument --- identification of $\Delta w = 1$ with
1 bit --- is the conceptual stipulation; the integrality itself is
external, established mathematics. \status{Q}

The identification $\Delta w = 1 \leftrightarrow$ 1 bit is the conceptual
bridge between topology and information theory. It is
\textbf{scale-independent}: Whether on the Planck scale or the cell scale ---
a winding quantum is a winding quantum. \status{B}

\begin{laierspur}
Imagine a string wrapped once around a post.
You can wrap it twice or three times --- but not one and a half times
without it coming undone. That is $\pi_1(T^4) \cong \mathbb{Z}^4$:
winding numbers are integers, enforced by the topology of the torus.
A bit corresponds to exactly one such winding.
\end{laierspur}

\section{Level 2: The Bit Energy --- Geometric, Scale-Dependent}

A winding quantum on a physical scale $L$ has an energy.
This energy follows from two known principles:

\paragraph{Step 1 --- Action of the winding quantum.}
The action of a winding quantum is $S = \Delta w \cdot h = h$.
That is a direct consequence of the quantization condition on $T^4$.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- Momentum from the uncertainty relation.}
On a spatial scale $L$, $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \sim \hbar$,
so $p \sim \hbar/L$.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- Energy of the winding quantum.}
With $E = pc$ (massless mode):
\[
  \boxed{E_\text{bit}(L) = \frac{\hbar c}{L}}
\]

That is the energy of a minimal information quantum on scale $L$.
It depends on the scale --- and only on it. \status{B}

\begin{laierspur}
The formula $\hbar c / L$ is familiar from physics: That is the energy
of a photon with wavelength $L$. A winding quantum on the torus behaves
energetically like such a photon --- its energy is determined by the
spatial scale on which it exists.
\end{laierspur}

\section{Level 3: Landauer as a Special Case --- Thermodynamic, One Scale}

Landauer's formula $E_L = k_BT\ln 2$ applies in the thermodynamic regime.
This is exactly the case when the geometric bit energy
coincides with the thermal energy of the bath:
\[
  E_\text{bit}(L) = k_BT\ln 2
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \frac{\hbar c}{L} = k_BT\ln 2
\]
Solving for the scale yields the \textbf{equivalence temperature}:
\[
  T_\text{equiv}(L) = \frac{\hbar c}{k_B \, L \, \ln 2}
\]
That is the temperature at which Landauer's formula applies for a given scale $L$
--- and \emph{only} at this temperature. \status{B}

\textbf{Proven \status{B}:} The hierarchy topology $\to$ geometry $\to$
thermodynamics is internally consistent. The derivation $E_\text{bit}(L) = \hbar c/L$
from winding quantization and uncertainty relation is mathematically correct.
Landauer's formula is a special case in this hierarchy.

\textbf{Derived and numerically verified \status{K}:} The equivalence temperature
matches biological room temperature at the cell scale.
The p-bit prediction $L_8 \approx 22$\,nm is consistent.

\textbf{Open \status{S}:} Is there a direct experimental test that
distinguishes $E_\text{bit}(L) = \hbar c/L$ from Landauer's $k_BT\ln 2$?
That would be an experiment on a scale where $T_\text{equiv}$ is far
from the bath temperature.
