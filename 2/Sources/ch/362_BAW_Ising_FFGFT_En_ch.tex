\chapter*{The BAW Ising Machine as a Resonance Computer}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The BAW Ising Machine as a Resonance Computer}

\begin{abstract}
Vadde, Ovcharov, González, Khymyn, Litvinenko and Åkerman (Commun.\
Phys.~9:290, 8~September 2026, Open Access) present a time-multiplexed
Ising machine with 2048 spins on two quartz bulk acoustic wave delay lines.
This document reads the work as a case study of the FFGFT classification of
resonance computers (Docs.~173, 328, 343): spins as phase states of a ring
oscillator, the Barkhausen condition as winding quantisation, parametric
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ selection, a coupling regime with a chaos threshold, and ---
most clearly in quantitative terms --- a resolution floor: 99.9\,\%
approximate solutions are found reliably, exact solutions ($E=0$) are not
as problem size grows. All quotations verbatim \QM. Status of the
classifications: \SM.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Source: What Vadde et al.\ Build}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Architecture}

\begin{quote}\QM
``We use two serially connected 20.5 MHz, 707~$\mu$s bulk acoustic wave
delay lines supporting 2048 spins. Our design provides all-to-all
connectivity with 15-bit coupling resolution and finds approximate MAX-CUT
solutions in 341~ms.'' (Abstract)
\end{quote}
The spins are not particles but phases of circulating RF pulses:
\begin{quote}\QM
``The spin states $+1$ and $-1$ are encoded in the relative phase of the
pulses, with bistable phases achieved by phase-binarizing the propagating
RF pulses using a phase-sensitive amplifier (PSA).'' (Section \emph{Design})
\end{quote}

\subsection{The ring oscillator and the Barkhausen condition}

\begin{quote}\QM
``The BAW delay line, together with linear amplifiers and the PSA, forms a
multi-physics ring oscillator. The key step of the design is to establish a
stable circulation of RF pulses within this loop. This requires satisfying
the Barkhausen stability criteria: the loop gain must be larger than one,
and the total phase shift around the loop must be an integer multiple of
$2\pi$.'' (Section \emph{Design})
\end{quote}

\subsection{Parametric binarisation}

\begin{quote}\QM
``The PSA further narrows the stability criteria to only those signals with
either phase 0 or $\pi$ relative to the pumping signal that is twice the
reference frequency.'' (Section \emph{Design})
\end{quote}

\subsection{Minimum cycles and pulse duration}

\begin{quote}\QM
``Due to the resonance mechanism underlying phase-sensitive amplification,
a minimum of 5--10 cycles per pulse is required, setting a lower bound on
the pulse duration to $\sim$250--500~ns. This constraint dominates and
effectively determines the pulse period.'' (Section \emph{Design})
\end{quote}

\subsection{Coupling strength and chaos threshold}

\begin{quote}\QM
``The amplitude of the coupling signal can be tuned using the potentiometer
of the DAC, allowing an additional control to ensure the injected signal
remains within 5--30\,\% of the circulating RF signal and does not overpower
it, avoiding chaotization of the oscillatory circuit.''
(Section \emph{Design})
\end{quote}
\begin{quote}\QM
``The performance of BAWIM depends on the coupling strength, and there
exists an optimal coupling strength depending on the problem instance.''
(Section \emph{Performance})
\end{quote}

\subsection{Approximation versus exactness}

MAX-CUT:
\begin{quote}\QM
``Here, we observe that BAWIM attains up to 95\,\% of HbSB's best
performance in terms of Ising energy, and up to 99.99\,\% in terms of the
MAX-CUT score.'' (Section \emph{Dependence on the edge density})
\end{quote}
Number partitioning:
\begin{quote}\QM
``Here, the exact solution corresponds to a difference of 0, while the
99.9\,\% approximate solution indicates that the difference is less than
0.1\,\% of the maximum possible value [\ldots]. In all cases, BAWIM
outperforms the HbSB algorithm, achieving a 100\,\% success rate in most
instances when considering the 99.9\,\% approximate solution.''
(Section \emph{Number partitioning})
\end{quote}
Figure~5(b) shows what the text does not spell out: the curve ``BAWIM
Exact'' falls from about 90\,\% success at 32 numbers to near zero at
2048 numbers, while ``BAWIM 99.9\,\%'' stays at 100\,\% across the whole
range.

Sudoku:
\begin{quote}\QM
``Notably, even when the Ising energy approaches the ground state, the
Sudoku solution remains far from correct. [\ldots] Sudoku requires exact
constraint satisfaction; therefore, a near-ground-state Ising energy may
still correspond to an invalid solution.'' (Section \emph{Sudoku})
\end{quote}

\subsection{Thermal stability}

\begin{quote}\QM
``In contrast, our BAWIM exhibits a temperature coefficient of phase
accumulation of only 780~deg/$^\circ$C, which is $2.21\times10^4$ times
lower than that of the CIM.'' (Section \emph{Comparison with CIMs})
\end{quote}

\section{Classification 1: Ising Machine = Resonator (Doc.~173)}
% ============================================================

Doc.~173 §\emph{The Arc Closes} says of the coherent Ising machine: ``This
resonator also has discrete states: it oscillates in either one or the
other phase --- spin-up or spin-down, zero or one. The excitation is
analogue --- continuous laser light, continuously adjustable coupling
strengths. The state is discrete.'' And in the conclusion: ``An Ising
machine is a resonator. All discrete from inside. All analogue from
outside.''

BAWIM is this description in its purest form --- no photonics, no
cryogenics. The states are phases 0 and $\pi$; the excitation is a
continuous RF carrier; the coupling is an analogue current with 15-bit
resolution. The spin is not an object but a phase relation. \SM

% ============================================================
\section{Classification 2: Barkhausen as Winding Quantisation (Doc.~314)}
% ============================================================

The Barkhausen condition ``integer multiple of $2\pi$'' is a winding
number: only states whose accumulated phase around the closed loop is
$2\pi n$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, circulate stably. \BM\ (Doc.~314, Ch.~D2):
``winding numbers are topological''; the mode structure on $T^4$ is fixed
by integer winding around every cycle.

The ring oscillator is an $S^1$ --- a single cycle. The Barkhausen
condition is winding quantisation on that $S^1$. BAWIM thus realises
technically what FFGFT posits for $T^4$: states exist only on integer
windings. \SM

% ============================================================
\section{Classification 3: Parametric $\mathbb{Z}_2$ Selection (Doc.~339)}
% ============================================================

The PSA is pumped at $2\omega$ and passes only phases $0$ and $\pi$. This is
period doubling: the phase state space $S^1$ is reduced to two points
$\{0,\pi\}\cong\{+1,-1\}$.

\BM\ (Doc.~339, §3.1): The fixed points $\{+1,-1\}$ of the Frobenius on
$GF(27)^*$ form the massive $\mathbb{Z}_2$ sector. \BM\ (Doc.~336): The
norm classes $\{+1,-1\}$ separate light from heavy.

BAWIM's parametric selection is a technical realisation of the same
dichotomy: from a continuous phase space exactly two fixed points are
filtered out. That there are \emph{two} and not three is due to pumping
at $2\omega$; pumping at $3\omega$ would yield a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ selection
$\{0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3\}$ --- the Potts machine that Whitehead et al.\
(Ref.~12 in Vadde et al.) built in CMOS. \SM

% ============================================================
\section{Classification 4: Coupling Regime with Chaos Threshold (Doc.~328)}
% ============================================================

Doc.~328 treats coupling regimes of coupled oscillators: the Adler model,
synchronisation, phase locking, and the limit beyond which coupling
destroys the oscillator. BAWIM sets exactly this limit operationally:
injection at 5--30\,\% of the circulating signal, ``avoiding chaotization''.

The existence of an \emph{optimal} coupling strength per problem instance
(Suppl.\ Note~5) is the Adler sweet spot: too weak --- no synchronisation,
the system stays in random states; too strong --- chaotisation, phase
binarisation breaks down. In between lies the window in which the
oscillator bank settles into the energy minimum. \SM

% ============================================================
\section{Classification 5: The Resolution Floor (Doc.~343 §G$'$)}
% ============================================================

This is the classification with the clearest quantitative support.
Doc.~343 §G$'$ states: a resonance-based computer is capped at
$N_{\max}\approx1/\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon$ is the coupling
tolerance; it finds minima to tolerance but not the exact zero. \BM

BAWIM displays this behaviour in three forms:

\paragraph{MAX-CUT.} 95\,\% of Ising energy, 99.99\,\% of cut value. The
optimum is reached to within fractions of a percent --- but not exactly.

\paragraph{Number partitioning.} The difference
$E=|\sum_{A}a_i-\sum_{B}a_i|$ is brought below $0.1\,\%$ of the total sum
with 100\,\% success. The \emph{exact} partition $E=0$, by contrast, is
still found in $\sim$90\,\% of runs at 32 numbers and practically never at
2048 numbers (Fig.~5b). Coupling resolution is 15~bit; the integers are
$\le178$; the total sum at 2048 numbers is $\sim1.8\times10^5$. A tolerance
of 0.1\,\% corresponds to $\sim180$ --- the magnitude of a single number.
The machine solves the problem to within one element, not to zero.

\paragraph{Sudoku.} At 99.42\,\% of ground-state energy the solution is
still invalid. Sudoku is a constraint-satisfaction problem: there is no
``almost right''. Vadde et al.\ say so themselves: ``a near-ground-state
Ising energy may still correspond to an invalid solution.'' That BAWIM
nevertheless solves the Sudoku is due to the smallness of the problem (729
spins) and to the ground-state energy being an isolated minimum here; HbSB
fails on the same problem.

\paragraph{The classification.} All three findings are consistent with
Doc.~343: the resonance computer is an analogue computer with a resolution
floor. It is \emph{not} worse than a digital algorithm --- on
approximations it is better (NPP 99.9\,\%: BAWIM beats HbSB throughout).
It is \emph{different}: it finds the neighbourhood of the minimum, not the
point. For optimisation that is the right task; for exact arithmetic it is
not. \SM

\section{What Does Not Correspond}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Classical, not quantum.} BAWIM is a classical RF ring
oscillator at room temperature. It claims no quantum advantage and asserts
none. The FFGFT classifications concern resonator structure, not quantum
coherence.

\paragraph{One cycle, not four.} The ring oscillator is $S^1$; $T^4$ has
four independent cycles. The winding parallel (Classification~2) holds for
one cycle; the $T^4$-specific statements (fixed points, $\mathbb{Z}_3$
triality, mass assignment) have no counterpart in BAWIM.

\paragraph{Benchmark caveat.} Vadde et al.\ stress twice that no parameter
optimisation was performed for either HbSB or BAWIM. The quantitative
comparisons (341~ms versus 102~ms; NPP success rates) are therefore
snapshots. The \emph{qualitative} structure --- approximation reliable,
exactness scale-dependent --- is unaffected.

\paragraph{The resolution floor here is technical, not fundamental.} The
15-bit coupling resolution is an electronics choice (AD9708 DAC), not a law
of nature. Doc.~343 §G$'$ speaks of $\varepsilon=\xi$ as the natural floor;
BAWIM's $2^{-15}\approx3\times10^{-5}$ happens to lie in the same order of
magnitude as $\xi=1.33\times10^{-4}$, without any connection being claimed
here.

BAWIM is the clearest published case to date of a resonance computer in
the sense of Docs.~173/343: thermally stable, without post-selection,
without cryogenics, with reproducible approximate solutions and a
measurable resolution floor for exact ones. It confirms the FFGFT
classification not as a theory but as a taxonomy: what resonates finds
minima --- and finds them to tolerance.
