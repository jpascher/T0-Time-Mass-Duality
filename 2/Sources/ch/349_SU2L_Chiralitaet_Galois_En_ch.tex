\section*{Abstract}
We examine whether the SU$(2)_L$ chirality structure of the Standard Model follows from the Cl$(6)$ grade parity established in Doc.~341. The pseudoscalar $\gamma_5=e_1e_2e_3e_4e_5e_6$ acts on grade~$r$ as the grade involution, $\gamma_5 X\gamma_5^{-1}=(-1)^r X$; this is not an eigenvalue equation on blades, and in Cl$(6,0)$, $\gamma_5^2=-1$. Chirality has to be captured by a projector $P_\pm=(1\pm i\gamma_5)/2$ in the complexification. The Su/Sd split (Doc.~344) is therefore not directly the right/left split: by Doc.~344, Su contains even and odd $N$ values, Sd[0] is the anti-neutrino, and Cl$(6)$ has no grade~7 for $e_L$. The SU$(2)_L$ chirality structure left open in Docs.~347/348 remains open \SM.
\section*{Background}
Doc.~344 \BM: the jE7 projector separates the Su and Sd sectors, with $N(\mathrm{Su})=[0,1,1,1,2,2,2,3]$ (even and odd values) and negative $N(\mathrm{Sd})$; Su[1--3] $=$ anti-down, Su[4--6] $=$ up, Su[7] $=$ positron, Sd[0] $=$ anti-neutrino, Sd[7] $=$ electron. A split ``Su: $N$ even, Sd: $N$ odd'' does not exist; indexing and $N$ values below follow Doc.~344.
\section{Theorem A --- Grade parity and $\gamma_5$ \KM}
$\gamma_5 X\gamma_5^{-1}=(-1)^r X$ for a grade-$r$ element $X\in\mathrm{Cl}(6)$, since $\gamma_5$ anticommutes with every generator $e_i$, so that $\gamma_5 X=(-1)^r X\gamma_5$ for a grade-$r$ blade. \KM
This is a conjugation statement (grade involution), not an eigenvalue equation on blades: left multiplication by $\gamma_5$ maps grade~$r$ to grade~$6-r$ (e.g.\ $\gamma_5\cdot 1=\gamma_5\neq 1$). Moreover, in Cl$(6,0)$ with $e_i^2=+1$: $\gamma_5^2=(-1)^{15}=-1$. Chirality has to be captured via conjugation or a projector, e.g.\ $P_\pm=(1\pm i\gamma_5)/2$ in the complexification since $(i\gamma_5)^2=+1$. An assignment ``even grades $=$ right-handed, odd grades $=$ left-handed'' as a $\gamma_5$ eigenvalue on blades therefore does not hold.
\section{Theorem B --- Su sector and Cl$(6)$ grades}
``Su $=$ even grades $=$ right-handed'' does not hold: by Doc.~344 the Su sector contains even and odd $N$ values, and even read by $N$ classes, $|N|=3$ in Su is the positron (not $e_R$). In addition, by Theorem~A the grade carries no $\gamma_5$ eigenvalue.
\section{Theorem C --- Sd sector and Cl$(6)$ grades}
Likewise ``Sd $=$ odd grades $=$ left-handed'' does not hold: by Doc.~344, Sd[0] is the anti-neutrino and Sd[7] the electron, and read by $N$ classes, $|N|=2$ in Sd is anti-up (not $\bar d$). Cl$(6)$ has only grades 0 to 6 with $1,6,15,20,15,6,1$ blades, $2^6$ in total; there is no grade~7, not even as a multigrade. An assignment grade $=2N+1$ therefore breaks down at $N=3$; the assignment of $e_L$ is open \SM.
\section{Theorem D --- SU$(2)_L$ doublets (open)}
A doublet assignment $(\nu_L,e_L)=(\mathrm{Sd}[0],\mathrm{Sd}[3])$ does not hold, because Sd[0] is the anti-neutrino by Doc.~344 and the assignment of $e_L$ is open (Theorem~C). Whether SU$(2)_L$ acts on exactly one of the two sectors, and which states form left-handed doublets and right-handed singlets, remains open \SM; the question has to be posed with the projector $P_\pm$ (Theorem~A) and with the indexing and $N$ values of Doc.~344.
\section{Theorem E --- $\mathbb{Z}_3$ fixed points/orbit and grade parity}
From Doc.~341: the $\sigma$ rotation separates $\mathrm{Vss}[0,2,4]$ ($\sigma$-fixed points, even grades) from $\mathrm{Vss}[1,3,5]$ ($\sigma$-three-orbit, odd grades) \BM. Identifying these classes with right- and left-handed presupposes the eigenvalue reading of the grade parity and, by Theorem~A, does not hold in this form. Whether the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry of T$^4$/Z$_3$ forces the chirality split via $P_\pm$ is open \SM.
