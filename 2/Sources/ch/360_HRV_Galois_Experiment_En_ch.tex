\section*{Abstract}

Docs.~358 and 359 provide the algebraic justification for a
Galois-informed approach to HRV frequency analysis. The present
document implements this approach as a Python module and validates
it on two public ECG datasets. The outcome is a
\emph{negative validation} with a clear structural reason.

\textbf{Results:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Module} \KM: \texttt{galois\_hrv\_preprocess.py} ---
  complete pipeline: Galois projection, resolution filter
  $100\xi\approx1.33\,\%$, HRV band limits as constants,
  orbit-type classification, Arnold tongue width.
\item \textbf{BIDMC-01} \LM: ICU patient, 88~y, 8~min PPG/ECG,
  Pimentel et al.\ 2017. Result: 0/3 ratios 5-limit,
  LF/VLF not resolvable (too short). Structural reason: recording
  too short for VLF, DFA $\alpha\approx0.5$ (white noise,
  fractal regulation lost).
\item \textbf{Fantasia} \LM: 5 young + 5 elderly healthy subjects,
  each $\approx120$~min at rest, spontaneous breathing, Gold et al.\ 2002.
  Result: LF peak CV 11--33\,\% $\gg$ 1.33\,\%, 5-limit LF/HF 4/10 (40\,\%),
  no group difference young/elderly.
\item \textbf{Structural reason} \BM: Under spontaneous breathing,
  $\text{HF} = \text{respiratory rate}$ (variable). LF/HF is the
  ratio of two independent control loops and not a fixed
  Galois ratio.
\item \textbf{Next step} \SM: Controlled breathing at 6/min
  (resonance-frequency protocol, Lehrer \& Gevirtz 2014) with a
  Polar H10 chest strap.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Galois-HRV Preprocessing Module}
% ============================================================

\texttt{galois\_hrv\_preprocess.py} implements (Doc.~359 §5):

\begin{description}
\item[Galois grid] 901 rational ratios $p/q$ with prime factors
  $\subseteq\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}$ up to denominator 60.
\item[Resolution filter] $\Delta f/f \geq 100\xi \approx 1.33\,\%$
  (Farey $Q_c\approx11$, Doc.~343).
\item[HRV band limits] VLF: $[0,\,\tfrac{1}{25}]$~Hz, LF: $[\tfrac{1}{25},\,\tfrac{3}{20}]$~Hz,
  HF: $[\tfrac{3}{20},\,\tfrac{2}{5}]$~Hz --- 5-limit constants \BM\ (Doc.~359 Theorem~A).
\item[Galois projection] LF/HF $\mapsto$ nearest $p/q$ in the grid.
\item[Orbit type] Galois depth $k=\mathrm{ord}_p(3)$ of the prime factors
  (Docs.~343, 358).
\item[Stability] Arnold tongue width $\approx 2/(p+q)$ (Doc.~328).
\item[Pipeline] \texttt{hrv\_galois\_pipeline(rr\_ms)} --- a single function call.
\end{description}

\section{Negative Validation I: BIDMC-01}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Dataset:} BIDMC PPG and Respiration Dataset v1.0.0,
Record 01 \LM\,\cite{Pimentel2017}.
88~y, male, MICU, 8~min, 125~Hz PPG + respiration.

\textbf{Result:} 0/3 frequency ratios satisfy the 5-limit criterion
(Welch and Lomb--Scargle). VLF not resolvable (recording length 8~min $<$ 15~min
minimum for VLF). DFA $\alpha_1=0.50$, $\alpha_2=0.45$ --- white noise;
in healthy subjects $\approx1.0$.

\textbf{Why:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Too short for VLF ($<15$~min).
\item ICU patient: fractal regulation largely lost.
\item HF peak = measured respiratory rate (0.328~Hz vs.\ RESP channel 0.336~Hz).
\item Measurement uncertainty Welch vs.\ Lomb--Scargle: $\pm2\,\%$ $>$ FFGFT limit
  1.33\,\%.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Structural Reason and Consequence}
% ============================================================

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Finding.} \BM\ Under spontaneous breathing, HF = respiratory rate
(variable, 0.15--0.40~Hz). LF/HF is the ratio of two
independent physiological control loops (baroreceptor~/ respiration)
and does not converge to a fixed Galois ratio.
Under spontaneous breathing the Galois test is structurally untestable.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Next step \SM:} Controlled breathing at 6~breaths/min
(0.10~Hz = LF resonance frequency). HF and LF then coincide;
the system may lock in (broad Arnold tongue) or it may not.
This is the Lehrer--Gevirtz effect \LM\,\cite{Lehrer2014},
which is physiologically well established.

\textbf{Recommended setup:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Polar H10 chest strap (1~ms RR resolution, $\approx90$~€).
\item 5~min acclimatisation, 10~min at 6/min with a metronome.
\item Export via Elite HRV or Kubios HRV (CSV).
\item Analysis with \texttt{galois\_hrv\_preprocess.py}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Breathing-Rate Scan: Results after Artefact Correction (10 September 2026)}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Protocol:} Polar H10 ECG chest strap, 130~Hz, seated, each ~5--6~min,
metronome click = phase change (4~bpm $\to$ 2/min; 8~bpm $\to$ 4/min;
10~bpm $\to$ 5/min; 12~bpm $\to$ 6/min).
R-peak detection with artefact filtering (median $\pm30\,\%$).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Condition & Breaths/min & LF~Hz & RMSSD~ms & Artefacts \\
\midrule
Spontaneous (supine) & 12.6 & 0.073 & 33.6 & 0 \\
4/min & 4.0 & 0.105 & 33.6 & 0 \\
2/min Rec.\,1 & 2.0 & 0.071 & 27.7 & 2 \\
2/min Rec.\,2 & 2.0 & 0.075 & 30.5 & 0 \\
5/min Rec.\,1 & 5.1 & 0.144 & 29.5 & 2 \\
\textbf{5/min Rec.\,2} & \textbf{5.0} & \textbf{0.132} & \textbf{39.9} & \textbf{0} \\
6/min & 6.0 & 0.040 & 36.1 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Finding 1 --- No RMSSD resonance effect:}
After artefact cleaning, RMSSD lies between 27 and 40~ms in all
conditions --- no significant difference.
Without artefact filtering, an apparent RMSSD maximum appears at 5/min
(67.2~ms); it is an R-peak detection artefact (2 missed beats caused by
amplitude modulation of the ECG during slow breathing,
$|\Delta\text{RR}|_\text{max}=662$~ms).

\textbf{Finding 2 --- The LF peak moves with the breathing rate:}
The LF peak shifts with the breathing rate (0.040--0.144~Hz),
but shows no stable resonance in the classical sense ($n=1$).
At 2/min the LF peak lies close to the spontaneous value (0.073~Hz) ---
at very slow breathing the baroreceptor eigenfrequency dominates.

\textbf{Galois:} LF/HF hits the grid at 5/min ($88/35$, 0.02~\%),
6/min ($39/35$, 0.06~\%) and spontaneous ($56/45$, 0.14~\%) --- all with $k=6$.
In no condition does LF coincide with the breathing rate.

\textbf{Methodological lessons:} Artefact filtering is mandatory under
controlled breathing. Short recordings ($n=1$) permit no statistical
conclusions --- all findings are exploratory.

\section{Validation III: Polar H10 Self-Test (10 September 2026)}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Device:} Polar H10 ECG chest strap, firmware 4.2.0, JSONL export
via Polar Sensor Recording (Android). Spontaneous breathing, supine,
21.9~min, 1517 RR intervals.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrl@{}}
\toprule
Parameter & Value & Assessment \\
\midrule
HR & 69.2~bpm & Normal at rest \\
RMSSD & 36.3~ms & Good for 75~y (Fantasia elderly: 20--35~ms) \\
SDNN & 53.8~ms & Normal \\
Breathing rate & 12.6/min & Spontaneous \\
LF peak & 0.073~Hz & Below norm ($\approx$0.10~Hz) \\
LF/HF & 1.2427 & $\to$ Galois $56/45$, distance 0.14~\% \\
LF CV & 10.55~\% & $\gg$ 1.33~\% (unstable) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Finding:} Confirms the Fantasia negative validation. The LF CV of
10.55~\% lies within the range of the Fantasia subjects (11--33~\%).
LF/HF lies in the Galois grid (distance 0.14~\%), but with orbit depth
$k=6$ (prime~7, GF$(729)$, R115) and low Arnold stability (0.020).

\textbf{Positive finding:} An RMSSD of 36.3~ms is very good for 75~y ---
clearly above the mean of the Fantasia ``elderly'' group (20--35~ms).
Data quality of the Polar H10: flawless.

\textbf{Note on the individual resonance frequency:} The LF peak at 0.073~Hz
(below the norm of 0.10~Hz) suggests that the individual
resonance frequency may lie at 4--5/min rather than at 6/min.
The individual protocol (testing rates of 4--8/min step by step)
would clarify this --- Lehrer \& Gevirtz (2014) describe the
determination procedure.
