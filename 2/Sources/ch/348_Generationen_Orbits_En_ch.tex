\section*{Abstract}The four irreducible polynomials $f_1,\ldots,f_4$ over GF$(3)$ define the physical orbits in GF$(27)^*$. Their root orbits are genuine Frobenius orbits under $k\mapsto3k\bmod26$; the three elements correspond to the three generations. The trace bilinear form $\langle a,b\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3)}(ab)$ gives the coupling structure: $f_3\times f_4$ (charged leptons $\times$ quarks) is a permutation matrix \BM; $f_1\times f_3$ (neutrinos $\times$ charged leptons) has normalised entries $1/\sqrt{2}$ \BM. Mixing angles (CKM, PMNS) themselves remain \SM.

\section{Why CKM mixing angles are complex}

The CKM matrix $V_\mathrm{CKM}$ is unitary with four independent parameters
(Wolfenstein parametrisation):
$\lambda\approx0.225$, $A\approx0.811$, $\rho\approx0.124$, $\eta\approx0.356$.
The CP-violating phase $\delta_{CP}\approx68°$ ($e^{i\delta_{CP}}=0.375+0.927i$)
makes the matrix essentially complex. It is responsible for CP violation in
kaon and B-meson decays (observed 1964 and 2001 respectively).

\subsection*{What GF$(27)^*$ provides and what it does not}

$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3))=\mathbb{Z}_3$ has three irreducible
representations over $\mathbb{C}$: trivial ($1$), $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ ($120°$),
$\omega^2$ ($240°$). If the coupling matrix transforms under $\rho_1$, a complex
phase arises --- the most plausible Galois candidate for the CP sector.

\textbf{The problem:} $\omega$ corresponds to $120°$, not $68°$ (gap $52°$) ---
no algebraically forced candidate.

\subsection*{Complete structure of the open problem \SM}

Three steps are needed for the full CKM matrix from GF$(27)$:
(1)~\textbf{Real zero structure} --- which couplings vanish: the trace bilinear
form gives the permutation matrix (Theorem~B) \BM.
(2)~\textbf{Mixing angles} --- corrections to the permutation matrix giving
$\lambda\approx0.225$, $A\approx0.811$: no forced candidate in GF$(27)$ \SM.
(3)~\textbf{CP phase} --- a complex embedding of the Galois group forcing
$\delta_{CP}\approx68°$: $\mathbb{Z}_3$ phases give $120°$, not $68°$ \SM.

\textbf{Core problem:} GF$(27)^*$ is a finite field over GF$(3)$ with no
natural complex structure. The trace bilinear form gives values in
$\{0,1,2\}\subset\mathrm{GF}(3)$. A complex embedding forcing
$\delta_{CP}=68°$ does not currently exist \SM.
