\section*{Abstract}

The four irreducible polynomials $f_1,\ldots,f_4$ over GF$(3)$ define the
physical orbits in GF$(27)^*$. Their root orbits are genuine Frobenius orbits
under $k\mapsto3k\bmod26$; the three elements correspond to the three
generations. The trace bilinear form $\langle a,b\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3)}(ab)$
gives the coupling structure between sectors: $f_3\times f_4$ (charged leptons
$\times$ quarks) is a permutation matrix \BM; $f_1\times f_3$ (neutrinos $\times$
charged leptons) has normalised entries $1/\sqrt{2}$ \BM. Mixing angles (CKM,
PMNS) remain \SM; the reason for the complexity of the CKM matrix is analysed.

\begin{theorem}[Frobenius cycles of root orbits \BM]
The root orbits are closed under Frobenius $k\mapsto3k\bmod26$:
$f_1:\ 1\to3\to9\to1$; $f_2:\ 17\to25\to23\to17$;
$f_3:\ 5\to15\to19\to5$; $f_4:\ 7\to21\to11\to7$.
The three elements of each orbit correspond to the three generations.
\end{theorem}

\section{Trace bilinear form}

\[
  \langle g^k, g^j\rangle = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3)}(g^{k+j})
  = g^{k+j}+g^{3(k+j)}+g^{9(k+j)}\in\mathrm{GF}(3).
\]
Values in $\{0,1,2\}$; as real numbers: $0\mapsto0$, $1\mapsto+1$, $2\mapsto-1$.

\section{Theorem B --- Coupling matrix $f_3\times f_4$ (CKM sector)}

\begin{theorem}[Permutation matrix in the quark-lepton sector \BM]
The coupling matrix between charged leptons ($f_3$) and quarks ($f_4$):
\[
  M^{(34)} = \begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&0&-1\\0&-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{theorem}

This is a permutation matrix: lepton Gen.~1 couples to quark Gen.~1; lepton
Gen.~2 to quark Gen.~3; lepton Gen.~3 to quark Gen.~2. The swap
Gen.~2$\leftrightarrow$Gen.~3 is algebraically forced \BM.

The experimental CKM matrix is nearly diagonal with small mixing angles. The
Galois coupling matrix gives the zero structure (which Gen.\ combinations do
not couple), but not the mixing angles themselves. \SM

\section{Theorem C --- Coupling matrix $f_1\times f_3$ (PMNS sector)}

\begin{theorem}[Equal-weight coupling in the neutrino sector \BM]
\[
  M^{(13)}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\\0&1&-1\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad |M^{(13)}_{ij}|_\mathrm{norm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
  \text{ for all non-zero entries.}
\]
\end{theorem}

Each neutrino generation couples to two charged lepton generations with weight
$1/\sqrt{2}$ --- consistent with the large PMNS mixing angles
($\theta_{23}\approx45°$, $\sin^2\theta_{23}\approx0.5\approx(1/\sqrt{2})^2$). \SM

\section{Why CKM mixing angles are complex}

The CKM matrix has four independent parameters (Wolfenstein):
$\lambda\approx0.225$, $A\approx0.811$, $\rho\approx0.124$, $\eta\approx0.356$.
The CP-violating phase $\delta_{CP}\approx68°$ ($e^{i\delta_{CP}}=0.375+0.927i$)
makes the matrix essentially complex (CP violation in kaon and B-meson decays,
observed 1964 and 2001).

\textbf{What GF$(27)^*$ provides and what it does not.}
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{GF}(27)/\mathrm{GF}(3))=\mathbb{Z}_3$ has three irreducible
representations over $\mathbb{C}$: trivial ($1$), $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ ($120°$),
$\omega^2$ ($240°$). If the coupling matrix transforms under $\rho_1$, a complex
phase arises --- the most plausible Galois candidate for the CP sector.
\textbf{Problem:} $\omega$ gives $120°$, not $68°$ (gap $52°$). No algebraically
forced candidate \SM.

\textbf{Three steps needed for full CKM from GF$(27)$:}
(1)~\textbf{Real zero structure}: permutation matrix from trace bilinear form \BM.
(2)~\textbf{Mixing angles}: corrections to permutation matrix giving $\lambda\approx0.225$,
$A\approx0.811$. Three checked paths: direct $\xi$-corrections ($\sin\theta_C/\xi\approx1685$,
too large); $\xi^{|k_i-k_j|}$ weighting ($\sim10^{-8}$, too small); Galois quotients
$2/9\approx0.222$ and $3/13\approx0.231$ (near, but not forced). No candidate \SM.
(3)~\textbf{CP phase}: complex embedding of Galois group forcing $\delta_{CP}\approx68°$.
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ phases give $120°$, not $68°$. GF$(27)^*$ has no natural complex
structure; the trace bilinear form gives values in $\{0,1,2\}\subset\mathrm{GF}(3)$ \SM.

\textbf{Important distinction: not derivable $\neq$ not usable.}
$\delta_{CP}\approx68°$ can be set as a phase anchor --- structurally identical to
$v=246$~GeV or $C_\mathrm{conv}$: empirically given, not freely adjustable, and once
set all CP-violating observables are fully determined. Deriving $\delta_{CP}$ from
first principles remains \SM; computing with it is immediately possible.
