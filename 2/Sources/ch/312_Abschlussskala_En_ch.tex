\section{Half the booking [K]}

$\tilde T \cdot m = 1$ holds fundamentally \emph{statically}: it is
a constitutive relation, not an equation of motion. Time dilation
is nevertheless almost always told dynamically -- \enquote{the
clock in the potential is slowed down, the photon loses energy on
the way up}. This telling forgets the mass side of the booking.

The static counter-account is well established (Okun, Selivanov,
Telegdi 2000): in a static gravitational field the photon frequency

invariance; the frequency is a conserved quantity). What differs
are the \emph{atoms} at different locations -- their transition
energies, i.e.\ effectively their energy/mass levels in the
potential. The redshift arises in the \emph{comparison of two
different clocks}, not through anything that \enquote{happens} to
the photon en route.

In FFGFT language: $\tilde T(x)\cdot m(x) = 1$ holds at every
point; \enquote{time dilation} is the static \textbf{map} $m(x)$,
