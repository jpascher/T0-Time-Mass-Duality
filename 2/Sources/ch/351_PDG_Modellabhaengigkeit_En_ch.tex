\section*{Abstract}

FFGFT predictions are compared against PDG and CODATA values. This document examines how those comparison values actually arise: which raw observable underlies each, which theory (QED, Standard Model) enters the extraction, and where irrational numbers already appear in the extraction formulae themselves.

Main findings: (1)~$m_e$ and $m_\mu/m_e$ are the least model-dependent anchors, but $m_\mu/m_e$ is QED-dependent and derives from a 27-year-old muonium HFS measurement whose theory uncertainty CODATA underestimates by a factor of 5--10 (Eides 2026). (2)~$\alpha$ carries an unresolved $5\sigma$ tension between the two best recoil measurements. (3)~$v=246$~GeV is never measured; it is the SM definition $v\equiv(\sqrt2\,G_F)^{-1/2}$. (4)~$G_F$ carries a theory correction $\Delta q=-0.44\,\%$ with a $4.7\sigma$ hadronic tension. (5)~$m_\tau$ has two measurement methods differing by 0.18~MeV; FFGFT lies $0.4\sigma$ from the PDG average. (6)~The magnitude of all theory uncertainties ($10^{-7}$ to $10^{-10}$) does not explain the $\sim1\,\%$ residual in $(m_\mu/m_e)^2$ --- it remains physical. (7)~Errors accumulate along the derivation chain; every prediction requires its full provenance path.

All 21 primary sources were verified on 5~September 2026.

\section{The question}

The corpus compares FFGFT predictions with PDG values and finds residuals at the sub-percent to percent level. Are PDG values theory-neutral, or do they themselves depend on the Standard Model --- such that part of the residual arises on the extraction side rather than the FFGFT side?

The FFGFT foundational statement: only ratios free of irrational numbers are exact; SI absolute values are structurally approximate (Docs.~241, 085). If PDG values themselves contain irrational factors in their extraction, the same limitation applies to the comparison side.

\section{How empirical values arise}

No PDG or CODATA value is a raw measurement. Each passes through the same chain:
\begin{center}
raw observable $\to$ theory formula $\to$ extracted parameter $\to$ CODATA adjustment $\to$ PDG value
\end{center}

\subsection{The CODATA adjustment}

CODATA publishes recommended values every four years, produced by a joint least-squares adjustment over roughly 300 input data linked by theory relations (QED, Rydberg formula, kinematics). The values are therefore \emph{correlated}: a change in one input shifts all dependent values. Where theory uncertainties enter, they are partly treated as adjustment parameters ($\delta_\text{th}$ in the observational equations) rather than as errors --- this is the Eides finding for muonium HFS \QM.

\subsection{Electron mass $m_e$}

\textbf{Raw observable:} cyclotron frequency of a hydrogen-like ion ($^{12}$C$^{5+}$, $^{28}$Si$^{13+}$, $^{20}$Ne$^{9+}$) in a Penning trap, and the Larmor precession frequency of the bound electron. The ratio is a pure number.

\textbf{Theory formula:} bound $g$-factor from QED bound-state theory. From the frequency ratio and $g_\text{bound}$ follows $m_e/m_\text{ion}$; with the ion mass in u follows $m_e$ in u; conversion to MeV via u\,$\to$\,kg\,$\to$\,MeV.

\textbf{Theory dependence:} QED for $g_\text{bound}$, small and well controlled. Current: Heiße et al.\ (2023) at 0.1~ppb \QM.

\subsection{Muon-electron mass ratio $m_\mu/m_e$}

\textbf{Raw observable:} ground-state hyperfine splitting of muonium --- a microwave resonance at 4.463~GHz --- and the Zeeman splitting in a magnetic field.

\textbf{Theory formula:} $\Delta\nu_\text{HFS}=\nu_F\,[1+\text{corrections}]$ with the Fermi frequency
\begin{equation}
\nu_F=\tfrac{16}{3}\,\alpha^2\,c\,R_\infty\,\frac{m_e}{m_\mu}\Big(\frac{m_r}{m_e}\Big)^3 .
\end{equation}
Corrections: QED to $O(\alpha^3)$, recoil in $m_e/m_\mu$, weak $Z$ exchange, hadronic vacuum polarisation.

\textbf{Theory dependence:} high. Sole precision measurement: LAMPF 1999 (Liu et al.). Eides (2026) shows that CODATA lists the measured value 4\,463\,302\,776(51)~Hz as the theoretical value; the actual theory uncertainty is 271--515~Hz \QM.

\textbf{For FFGFT:} $\nu_F\propto\alpha^2$. The anchor $m_\mu/m_e$ implicitly contains $\alpha$. Anchor and $\alpha$ are not independent.

\subsection{Fine-structure constant $\alpha$}

Two routes that disagree at $5\sigma$ \QM:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Atom recoil:} $\alpha^2=2R_\infty\,(h/m_\text{atom})\,(m_\text{atom}/m_e)/c$. Rb (Morel et al.\ 2020): $\alpha^{-1}=137.035\,999\,206(11)$. Cs (Parker et al.\ 2018): $137.035\,999\,046(27)$. Difference $1.6\times10^{-7}$, $>5\sigma$.
\item \textbf{Electron $g-2$:} $a_e$ at $10^{-13}$ (Fan et al.\ 2023) plus 5-loop QED (12\,672 diagrams, Aoyama et al.). Gives $\alpha^{-1}=137.035\,999\,166(15)$ --- between Rb and Cs.
\end{itemize}
The CODATA value 137.035\,999\,177(21) is an average covering none of the measurements. FFGFT: $\alpha^{-1}=3700/27=137.037\,037\ldots$ (Doc.~338, R101), deviation 7.6~ppm --- 100$\times$ larger than the Rb/Cs tension.

\textbf{Threshold scan:} $\sigma(e^+e^-\to\tau^+\tau^-)$ versus beam energy near $2m_\tau\approx3.55$~GeV; $m_\tau$ as fit parameter of the QED threshold curve. \textbf{Pseudomass:} at 10.6~GeV, the kinematic edge of the invariant mass in $\tau\to3\pi\nu$; assumes $m_{\nu_\tau}=0$.

\textbf{For FFGFT:} $m_\tau=1776.97$~MeV (pred) lies 0.04~MeV $=0.4\sigma$ from the PDG average. The methods differ by 0.18~MeV. The falsification test is undecided; Belle II with $>1$~ab$^{-1}$ should reach $\sigma<0.05$~MeV.

\subsection{Fermi constant $G_F$ and vacuum expectation value $v$}

\textbf{Raw observable for $G_F$:} muon decay time distribution (MuLan, $10^{12}$ decays), $\tau_\mu=2.196\,980\,3(22)$~µs \QM.

\textbf{Theory formula} (Eberhart et al.\ 2026 \QM):
\begin{equation}
\tau_\mu^{-1}=\frac{G_F^2\,m_\mu^5}{192\pi^3}\,(1+\Delta q),\qquad \Delta q=(-4\,384\,678\pm34)\times10^{-9}\approx-0.44\,\%.
\end{equation}
$\Delta q$ contains QED to $O(\alpha^3)$, hadronic vacuum polarisation, electron-mass effects. Purely electroweak corrections are absorbed into $G_F$ by definition (Sirlin 1980). The hadronic component carries a $4.7\sigma$ tension (CMD-3 vs.\ older data).

\textbf{Raw observable for $v$:} none. $v\equiv(\sqrt2\,G_F)^{-1/2}=246.22$~GeV follows from $M_W=gv/2$ and $G_F/\sqrt2=g^2/(8M_W^2)$. \textbf{$v$ exists only within the Standard Model.} Its numerical value is the muon lifetime in SM clothing.

\textbf{For FFGFT:} Doc.~006/R104 gives $v=248.3$~GeV (without $K_\text{frak}$) or 245.6~GeV (with $K_\text{frak}=74/75$). Comparison with 246.22~GeV is a comparison of \emph{FFGFT structure against SM definition}, not against an observable. Only $\tau_\mu$ and $m_\mu$ are measured.

\subsection{Gravitational constant $G$}

Torsion balance (Li et al.\ 2018, 11.6~ppm) or atom interferometry (Rosi et al.\ 2014, 150~ppm). Theory: Newton. The only model-free value, but the least precise: CODATA $6.67430(15)\times10^{-11}$, 22~ppm \QM.

\subsection{Neutrino mass-squared differences}

Event rates versus $L/E$, three-flavour fit (NuFIT 6.0 \QM). $\Delta m^2_{31}$ depends on whether SK atmospheric data are included: ratio 33.7 (with) vs.\ 33.9 (without). FFGFT $360/11=32.73$; measurement uncertainty 1--3\,\% $\approx$ FFGFT residual. Not discriminating.

\section{Irrational factors in the extraction formulae}

The FFGFT foundational statement --- ratios of rationals are exact, SI absolute values carry the approximation of the projection $S^1_m\to\mathbb R_t$ (Docs.~306, 333) --- applies to the SM extraction formulae themselves:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Formula & Irrational factor \\
\midrule
$G_F$ & $\tau_\mu^{-1}=G_F^2m_\mu^5/(192\pi^3)$ & $\pi^3$ \\
$v$ & $v=(\sqrt2\,G_F)^{-1/2}$ & $\sqrt2$ \\
$\alpha$ (recoil) & $\alpha^2=2R_\infty h/(mc)$ & $R_\infty\propto\alpha^2$: implicitly circular \\
HFS & $\nu_F\propto\alpha^2$ & $\alpha^2$ \\
$\Delta q$ & expansion in $\alpha/\pi$ & $\pi$ in every term \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

``$G_F$ measured'' means: $\tau_\mu$ measured, divided by $192\pi^3$, square root taken. ``$v$ measured'' additionally means: divided by $\sqrt2$, square root again. The irrational factors come from the SM formulation (phase-space integral, Higgs normalisation), not from measurement. The only convention-free comparison would be at the level of $\tau_\mu=2.196\,980\,3$~µs --- which FFGFT would have to predict directly \SM.

\section{The residual in $(m_\mu/m_e)^2$: exact computation}

FFGFT bare (Doc.~338): $(m_\mu/m_e)^2=43200$ exactly, an integer, $=|\text{GF}(9)^*|^2\cdot5^2\cdot|\text{GF}(27)|$. With CODATA 2022 in exact rational arithmetic:
\begin{align}
(m_\mu/m_e)^2_\text{PDG} &= 42753.12 \\
\text{residual} &= 446.88\quad(1.045\,\%) \\
\sigma_\text{rel}\big((m_\mu/m_e)^2\big) &= 4.35\times10^{-8}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{residual}=240\,000\,\sigma .
\end{align}

Possible error sources, computed:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item CODATA uncertainty: $\pm0.002$ --- factor $2\times10^5$ too small.
\item Eides correction to HFS theory uncertainty (515 instead of 51~Hz): $\pm0.010$ --- residual remains $45\,000\,\sigma$.
\item Choice of $\alpha$ in the HFS anchor (FFGFT $3700/27$ instead of CODATA): shifts $(m_\mu/m_e)^2$ by $-1.3$ --- factor 500 too small.
\item To reach 43200, $m_\mu$ would need to be 551~keV higher (measurement error 2.3~eV), or $m_e$ 2.65~keV lower (error 0.15~meV), or the HFS 23~MHz lower (error 51~Hz).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Conclusion \BM:} No measurement error explains the residual. It is physical and belongs to the theory: fractal-recursive correction (Docs.~295, 146, 338) and projection approximation (Docs.~306, 333). In the square, without square root: $43200\cdot(1-77.6\,\xi)=42753$; $K_\text{frak}=1-100\xi$ over-corrects by $22.4\,\xi$. Which power of $K_\text{frak}$ acts on the quadratic identity is not fixed in the corpus \SM.


\subsection*{Rational or irrational: where the comparison is clean}

The discussion above compared three Galois identities against PDG values.
Not all comparisons are equally clean:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Comparison & Galois value & Type & Residual \\
\midrule
$(m_\mu/m_e)^2$ vs.\ $43200$ & $43200$ (integer) & rational & $-1.034\,\%$ \\
$m_\mu/m_e$ vs.\ $\sqrt{43200}$ & $120\sqrt{3}$ & \textbf{irrational!} & $-0.519\,\%$ \\
$m_e\cdot m_\mu$ vs.\ $54~\text{MeV}^2$ & $54$ (integer) & rational & $-0.016\,\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The linear comparison $m_\mu/m_e$ vs.\ $\sqrt{43200}=120\sqrt{3}$ is \emph{not a
clean rational comparison}: $\sqrt{43200}$ is irrational, and the $-0.52\,\%$
residual is not an independent physical finding. It equals exactly half the
quadratic residual ($-1.034\,\%/2\approx-0.517\,\%$), because taking the square root
approximately halves the deviation. Nothing new becomes visible.

Only two comparisons are algebraically clean:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Square:} $(m_\mu/m_e)^2 = 43200$ --- both sides rational,
  residual $-1.034\,\%$ physical.
\item \textbf{Product:} $m_e\cdot m_\mu = 54~\text{MeV}^2$ --- both sides rational,
  residual $-0.016\,\%$ \KM{} (Doc.~338; algebraic origin:
  $|GF(3)^*|\cdot|GF(27)| = 2\cdot27 = 54$).
\end{itemize}

The product $m_e\cdot m_\mu = 54~\text{MeV}^2$ is the algebraically cleanest
Galois identity with the smallest rational residual ($-0.016\,\%$). The absolute
deviation is $-0.0087~\text{MeV}^2$ ($-7400\,\sigma$) --- also physical, but
65 times smaller than the quadratic residual.

\textbf{Bookkeeping rule:} Galois identities should be compared only in their natural
algebraic form --- where no irrational numbers appear. For $(m_\mu/m_e)^2 = 43200$
that is the square comparison; for $m_e\cdot m_\mu$ the product. The linear comparison
$m_\mu/m_e$ vs.\ $\sqrt{43200}$ should be avoided because it introduces an irrational
intermediate (verification script: \texttt{pruef\_351\_rationale\_vergleiche.py}).

\section{Error accumulation along the derivation chain}

Each step carries a small residual (recursion $\sim100\xi$, projection $\sim\xi$, extraction theory $10^{-7}$--$10^{-10}$). Already at $m_\mu$, the first step after the anchor $m_e$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Route $m_e\to m_\mu$ & Residual \\
\midrule
Galois bare: $m_\mu=\sqrt{43200}\,m_e$ & $+0.52\,\%$ \\
with $\sqrt{K_\text{frak}}$: $m_\mu=\sqrt{43200\cdot74/75}\,m_e$ & $-0.15\,\%$ \\
via $\alpha$: $E_0=\sqrt{\alpha/\xi}$, $m_\mu=E_0^2/m_e$ & $+1.37\,\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At $m_\tau$ (second step), $v$ (third, via $\xi^{3/2}$), each derivation accumulates further. To decide where a residual sits, convergence of empirical values onto the algebraic ones would have to be established --- it is not: $m_\mu/m_e$ (1999), $\alpha$ ($5\sigma$ tension), $\Delta m^2_\text{atm}$ ($2.4\sigma$ shift in 2024), $v$ (not measured).

\textbf{Bookkeeping:} For every reported prediction, state: (1)~the anchor, (2)~the number of derivation steps, (3)~corrections applied and where, (4)~the comparison value with its extraction method. ``FFGFT predicts $m_\mu$ to 0.5\,\%'' is incomplete; complete: ``From $m_e$ (PDG), in one step via the Galois identity, without $K_\text{frak}$, $m_\mu$ follows with $+0.52\,\%$ against the 1999 muonium HFS value.''

\section{Consequences for the corpus}

\textbf{No numerical value in the corpus changes.} What changes:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{$v$ is neither anchor nor observable.} Docs.~006, 319, R104: ``$v=246$~GeV (measured)'' $\to$ ``$v=246$~GeV (SM definition from $G_F$)''. Register R112.
\item \textbf{$m_\mu/m_e$ is QED-dependent.} ``model-independent'' $\to$ ``least model-dependent available value''; $\nu_F\propto\alpha^2$. Register R113.
\item \textbf{$\alpha$ has no unique measured value below 1~ppb.} Cite the comparison value as CODATA average with Rb/Cs tension.
\item \textbf{$m_\tau$:} two methods, PDG average 1776.93(9), FFGFT $0.4\sigma$ --- open.
\item \textbf{New open item \SM:} $\tau_\mu$ directly from FFGFT, without detour via $G_F$ and $v$ --- the only convention-free test of the weak sector.
\end{enumerate}
