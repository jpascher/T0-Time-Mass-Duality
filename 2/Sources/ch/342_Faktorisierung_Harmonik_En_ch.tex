\chapter*{Factorisation Classes in G$(6)$ and the Harmonics of the Primes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Factorisation Classes in G$(6)$ and the Harmonics of the Primes}

\begin{abstract}
Doc.~341 shows that GF$(27)$ enters G$(6)$ as an order structure through the
characteristic polynomial $\chi_X = (x^3+2x+1)^2(x+1)^2$. This document asks which
further factorisations $\chi$ admits in G$(6) \cong M_8(\text{GF}(9))$, and shows
that the same factorisation rule organises the harmonic patterns of the primes
(Doc.~057, Doc.~159).

Four results:
\textbf{(A)}~Over GF$(3)$ there are eight irreducible cubic polynomials, four of them
primitive (order~26) and four of order~13; they generate five factorisation classes
of $\chi$ in G$(6)$ \BM.
\textbf{(B)}~A prime~$p$ enters $|\text{GF}(3^k)^*|$ exactly at $k = \text{ord}_p(3)$;
the harmonic primes enter in the order $13\,(k{=}3)$, $5\,(k{=}4)$, $11\,(k{=}5)$,
$7\,(k{=}6)$, after which only large primes appear \BM.
\textbf{(C)}~Element orders in G$(6)$ are partitions of~8 with an lcm rule ---
the algebraic form of a chord of overtones \BM.
\textbf{(D)}~From $k \geq 4$ on, the Galois grid is finer than the fractal-recursive
correction ($1.05\,\%$, Doc.~338); Galois products then become statistically
indistinguishable from higher torus modes. This is not a contradiction but the
algebraic form of the mode convergence of Doc.~159 \BM.

Negative result: the tau sector is not a Galois product at $0.05\,\%$ \SM.
Check scripts: \texttt{pruef\_342\_faktorisierung\_primzahlen.py},
\texttt{pruef\_342b\_hoehere\_harmonien.py},
\texttt{pruef\_342c\_dichte\_hoehere\_harmonien.py}.
\end{abstract}

Docs.~338 and~341 showed that mass ratios from torus winding numbers $(r_i, p_i)$

$(m_\mu/m_e)^2 = 43200 = |\text{GF}(9)^*|^2 \cdot 5^2 \cdot |\text{GF}(27)|$.
The question whether the geometric formulation (vectors on $T^4$) is ``more precise''

orbifold $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$ (Doc.~314, Doc.~336).

The algebraic route has one methodological advantage: polynomial factorisation can
be enumerated systematically, geometric search cannot. This document exploits that.

\section{Theorem A: Factorisation classes in G$(6)$}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Irreducible cubic polynomials over GF$(3)$}

A monic cubic over GF$(3)$ is irreducible iff it has no root in $\{0,1,2\}$.
There are eight such polynomials; their companion matrices have order~26 or~13
in $\text{GL}_3(\text{GF}(3))$ \BM:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Irreducible cubics over GF$(3)$ and their order}
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
Label & Polynomial & Order \\
\midrule
$f_1$ & $x^3 + 2x + 1$ & 26 \\
$f_2$ & $x^3 + 2x^2 + 1$ & 26 \\
$f_3$ & $x^3 + 2x^2 + x + 1$ & 26 \\
$f_4$ & $x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 1$ & 26 \\
\midrule
$g_1$ & $x^3 + 2x + 2$ & 13 \\
$g_2$ & $x^3 + x^2 + 2$ & 13 \\
$g_3$ & $x^3 + x^2 + x + 2$ & 13 \\
$g_4$ & $x^3 + 2x^2 + 2x + 2$ & 13 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The number four is forced: GF$(27)^*$ has $\varphi(26) = 12$ primitive elements
in Frobenius orbits of length~3, hence $12/3 = 4$ minimal polynomials.
Doc.~341 uses $f_1$.

\subsection{The five classes}

In G$(6) \cong M_8(\text{GF}(9))$, $\chi$ splits over GF$(3)$ into factors whose
degrees sum to~8. With cubic and linear factors this gives five classes \BM:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{$\chi$ factorisation classes with GF$(27)$ content in G$(6)$}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Type & $\chi(x)$ & Count & Order \\
\midrule
Doc.-341 type & $f_i^{\,2}\,(x{+}1)^2$ & 4 & 26 \\
Mixed primitive & $f_i\, f_j\,(x{+}1)^2$, $i \neq j$ & 6 & 26 \\
Order-13 type & $g_i^{\,2}\,(x{+}1)^2$ & 4 & 13 (sign partners of the $f_i$, Doc.~343) \\
Primitive$\times$13 & $f_i\, g_j\,(x{+}1)^2$ & 16 & 26 \\
Thin & $f_i\,(x{+}1)^5$ & 4 & 26 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The four primitive polynomials are the four Frobenius conjugacy classes
$\{g, g^3, g^9\}$, $\{g^5, g^{15}, g^{45}\}$, $\{g^7, g^{21}, g^{63}\}$,
$\{g^{11}, g^{33}, g^{99}\}$ of a generator~$g$ of GF$(27)^*$. Assigning the
leptons to $f_1$ in Doc.~341 is a choice within this class; whether
$f_2, f_3, f_4$ carry further mass layers remains open \SM.

\section{Theorem B: Entry rule of the harmonic primes}
% ============================================================

Doc.~057 describes primes as elementary relational steps: $2$ octave, $3$ fifth,
$5$ major third, $7$ natural seventh, $11$ eleventh, $13$ thirteenth.
A $p$-limit system admits primes up to~$p$ as interval building blocks.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Order of GF$(3^k)^*$ and entry of new primes}
\begin{tabular}{rrlll}
\toprule
$k$ & $|\text{GF}(3^k)^*|$ & prime factors & new & harmonics \\
\midrule
1 & 2 & $2$ & 2 & octave \\
2 & 8 & $2^3$ & --- & GF$(9)$, Matzke's $\mathbb{Z}_3\mathbb{C}$ \\
3 & 26 & $2 \cdot 13$ & 13 & thirteenth; leptons (Doc.~338) \\
4 & 80 & $2^4 \cdot 5$ & 5 & major third; Doug's G$(3)$ orders \\
5 & 242 & $2 \cdot 11^2$ & 11 & eleventh; neutrinos (Doc.~340) \\
6 & 728 & $2^3 \cdot 7 \cdot 13$ & 7 & seventh; with 13 on one carrier \\
7 & 2186 & $2 \cdot 1093$ & 1093 & no small harmonics left \\
8 & 6560 & $2^5 \cdot 5 \cdot 41$ & 41 & --- \\
12 & 531440 & $2^4 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 13 \cdot 73$ & 73 & all four united \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Entry rule} \BM: A prime $p \neq 3$ divides $3^k - 1$ iff
$\text{ord}_p(3) \mid k$; it therefore first enters at $k = \text{ord}_p(3)$.
This yields the order $13, 5, 11, 7$ for $k = 3, 4, 5, 6$. All four harmonic primes
above~3 enter between $k=3$ and $k=6$; from $k=7$ on, only primes $\geq 41$ appear
(with $1093$, a Wieferich prime, at $k=7$).

This is the algebraic reason why practically relevant harmonics end at the
13-limit, and why the FFGFT corpus has so far needed only the primes
$\{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$. The neutrino eleven of Doc.~340
($\Delta m^2_\text{atm} = (11^2 - 1)\, m_\nu^2$) sits at $k = 5$; this had not
been located in the corpus before.

$\mathbb{Z}_3$ compatibility: for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, $\mathbb{Z}_3 \subset \text{GF}(p)^*$.
This holds for $7$ and $13$, not for $2, 5, 11$. The two $\mathbb{Z}_3$-compatible
primes are exactly those that appear as structural numbers in Doc.~289 (top quark)
and Docs.~338/341 (leptons) \BM.

\section{Theorem C: Chords as partitions of~8}
% ============================================================

For semisimple $X \in M_8(\text{GF}(9))$ with $\chi$ split over GF$(3)$ into factors
of degrees $d_1 + \cdots + d_r = 8$, the eigenvalues lie in GF$(3^{d_i})$ and
$\text{ord}(X)$ divides $\text{lcm}(3^{d_i} - 1)$ \BM. A partition of~8 is thus the
algebraic form of a chord of overtones $k \in \{d_i\}$; the order is the common period.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Partitions of~8 and maximal semisimple order}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
Partition & lcm$(3^{d_i}{-}1)$ & reading \\
\midrule
$(3,3,1,1)$ & 26 & pure GF$(27)$ chord (Doc.~341) \\
$(3,3,2)$ & 104 & $= 2^3 \cdot 13$: GF$(9)$ with GF$(27)$ \\
$(4,3,1)$ & 1040 & $= 2^4 \cdot 5 \cdot 13$: third with thirteenth \\
$(6,2)$, $(6,1,1)$ & 728 & $= 2^3 \cdot 7 \cdot 13$: seventh with thirteenth \\
$(5,3)$ & 3146 & $= 2 \cdot 11^2 \cdot 13$: eleventh with thirteenth \\
$(8)$ & 6560 & $= 2^5 \cdot 5 \cdot 41$: maximal \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The number of irreducible resp.\ primitive polynomials of degree~$k$ over GF$(3)$
grows quickly (Möbius formula): degree~3: $8/4$, degree~4: $18/8$, degree~6:
$116/48$, degree~8: $810/320$. The space of possible chords is therefore large ---
which leads directly to the question of discriminating power.

\section{Theorem D: Discriminating power and the boundary of the Galois layer}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Density of Galois products}

Forming all products of up to three building blocks
$\{3^k, 3^k - 1, \text{prime divisors}\}$ for $k \leq k_\text{max}$ with exponents
$\{-1, 1, 2\}$, the mean logarithmic spacing of neighbouring values in the window
$10 < x < 10^4$ drops as follows \BM:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Mean log spacing of Galois products}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$k_\text{max}$ & values in window & mean spacing & expected chance hits at $0.5\,\%$ \\
\midrule
2 & 54 & $12.8\,\%$ & 0.08 \\
3 & 218 & $3.17\,\%$ & 0.32 \\
4 & 650 & $1.06\,\%$ & 0.94 \\
5 & 1423 & $0.49\,\%$ & 2.06 \\
6 & 2503 & $0.28\,\%$ & 3.62 \\
8 & 5311 & $0.13\,\%$ & 7.69 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

From $k_\text{max} \approx 5$ on, the product set is denser than $0.5\,\%$: every
number is ``hit''. A control test confirms this: at $k_\text{max} = 6$, ten physical
mass ratios have 2.9 hits on average, two hundred random numbers 3.7. The hit rate
is at chance level \BM.

\subsection{The threshold: Galois grid versus fractal correction}

Doc.~338 quantifies the fractal-recursive higher-winding correction between bare
value and measurement at $1.05\,\%$ for $(m_\mu/m_e)^2$. The Galois grid is coarser
than twice this correction up to $k = 3$ ($3.17\,\% > 2.10\,\%$) and hence
discriminating; from $k = 4$ on it lies below ($1.06\,\% < 2.10\,\%$) \BM.

\textbf{Consequence.} The exact Galois layer ends structurally where the fractal
correction begins. Up to GF$(27)$ the algebra is exact and the $1\,\%$ is a
higher-mode correction; above that, the algebra itself is finer than the correction
and dissolves into the higher winding modes. Galois products with $k \geq 4$ are
statistically indistinguishable from higher torus modes.

This is not a contradiction. It is the same mechanism as in the overtone series:
the interval $(n{+}1) : n$ shrinks from 1200~cents ($n=1$) via 105~cents ($n=16$)
to below 5~cents at $n \approx 250$; the series becomes quasi-continuous. Doc.~159
describes exactly this mode convergence on the fractal torus; here it appears in
algebraic form.

\subsection{Methodological rule}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k \leq 3$ (GF$(3)$, GF$(9)$, GF$(27)$): identity layer. A hit with few
        building blocks is significant and may carry \KM/\BM\ if grounded
        geometrically in $\xi$ (as $43200$ in Doc.~338).
  \item $k = 4$ (GF$(81)$, 5-limit): transition region; only with additional
        justification.
  \item $k \geq 5$: no status from product search. A relation holds here only if
        derived geometrically from $\xi$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Negative result: tau sector}
% ============================================================

At a tolerance of $0.05\,\%$, none of the ratios $m_\tau/m_\mu$, $m_\tau/m_e$ or
their squares is a product of up to three Galois building blocks with $k \leq 6$
\SM. Hits at $0.5\,\%$ exist (e.g.\ $7 \cdot 11^2/3$ for $(m_\tau/m_\mu)^2$ at
$0.17\,\%$) but lie at chance level by Theorem~D. The tau ratio remains grounded in
the corpus through winding numbers (Doc.~258, Doc.~310), not through a Galois identity.
