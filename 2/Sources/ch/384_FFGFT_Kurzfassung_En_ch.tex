\chapter*{FFGFT in Brief}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{FFGFT in Brief}

\begin{abstract}
The Fundamental Fractal Geometric Field Theory (FFGFT) is first and foremost a ratio-based
theory. It works in natural units, $\hbar=c=1$, with the convention $\alpha=1$ and a
correspondingly redefined charge $e=\sqrt{4\pi}$. In this basic form there is exactly one
dimensionless parameter, $\xi=4/30000=1/7500$, and no fractal correction. Particles, couplings
and gravitation appear as properties of a compact four-dimensional carrier $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$;
the starting point is the time-mass duality $\tilde T\cdot m=1$. The guiding idea is resonance: particles are oscillation modes, mass ratios
are ratios of periods, $1/\xi=2^2\cdot3\cdot5^4$ is a point in Euler's Tonnetz, and the fractal
correction is the comma of the embedding into the continuum. The core statements are ratios:
$m_\mu/m_e$ and $m_\tau/m_\mu$ follow from $\xi$ to within $0.5$ and $1\,\%$, the electroweak
scale relates to the Planck energy as $\xi^4/(5\pi)$, the minimal length and minimal time relate
to the Planck quantities as $\xi$, the neutrino mass differences agree to $1\,\%$, and charge
quantisation follows algebraically from the Galois structure of $\mathrm{GF}(27)^*$. The golden
ratio enters through the icosahedron: the weights of the fivefold rotation stand exactly as
$\varphi^8$ and $2\varphi^4$, and $m_\tau/m_e=(74+\tfrac1{45})\varphi^8$ agrees to
$3\times10^{-5}$, within the measurement uncertainty. Only the
translation into SI needs a measured value as anchor, the factor $K_{\text{frak}}=74/75$ and
conversion quantities such as $C_{\text{conv}}$; this is also where the absolute values of
$\alpha$ and $G$ arise. This document summarises the corpus (Docs.~001--388, A series) as of
1~October 2026, after the complete recalculation. For every result it gives the formula, the
numerical value, the comparison value, the status and the documents that carry it, and it states
just as clearly what is set by choice, what remains open and what no longer holds. The cosmic
sector ($H_0$, $\Lambda$) is deliberately set aside.
\end{abstract}

\section{What FFGFT is}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Ratios in natural units}

The core of FFGFT is ratios. In natural units, mass, energy, temperature and inverse time are one
quantity in four guises (A085), and every physical statement can be written as a dimensionless
ratio: mass ratios, scale ratios to the Planck quantities, mixing angles, charge ratios. Such
ratios do not depend on any choice of units and carry the content of the theory. Absolute values
in SI are a second layer: they need a measured anchor and conversion quantities, and corrections
appear there that do not occur in the ratios (A080, A130, Doc.~122). This summary follows that
order: first the basic form and the ratios, then the translation into SI.

\subsection{Architecture in four layers}

FFGFT rests on three explicitly chosen basic assumptions (R56, Doc.~365): the time-mass duality
$\tilde T\cdot m=1$, the compact carrier $T^4$ and the identification with order~3,
$\mathbb{Z}_3$. A fourth, motivated structural choice is the lattice $D_4$; Doc.~380 shows that
among the comparison carriers tested with a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ action that has fixed points, $D_4$ has
the lowest lattice energy \KM, but the choice remains a choice \SM. In addition there is exactly
one dimensionless parameter, $\xi=4/30000$. The fine-structure constant is set to $\alpha=1$ by
adjusting the charge unit; this is a convention, not a result \QM. Galois fields, Hodge theory
and representation theory serve as consistency checks, not as additional inputs (Doc.~365,
layer~4). The spacetime dimension $3+1$ is an empirical input (P34).

\subsection{Resonance as the guiding idea: Euler's Tonnetz}

Behind all individual results stands one idea: whatever exists needs resonance. A particle is an
oscillation mode on the compact carrier with integer winding numbers (resonance condition,
Doc.~189); through $\tilde T\cdot m=1$ every mass becomes a frequency and every mass ratio a ratio
of periods. Ratios of periods are characters of cyclic groups, and the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ reduction
turns them into arithmetic in $\mathrm{GF}(3^k)$: harmonics and Galois classification are not
analogous but two representations of the same object (Doc.~358, Theorem~D \BM). What stands
above is thus the geometry that possesses resonances; it fixes all ratios. The measured value is
the concert pitch: it says \emph{where} the structure sounds, not \emph{how} (Doc.~310; on the
anchor, Section~4).

The model is Euler's Tonnetz (1739), the lattice of just intonation with the primes $2$, $3$, $5$
as axes. It carries the theory at three points:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{$\xi$ is a lattice point.} $1/\xi=7500=2^2\cdot3\cdot5^4$ lies in the 5-limit space
  at the coordinates $(-2,-1,-4)$ (Doc.~316 \KM). Seven of the nine Yukawa prefactors are 5-limit
  ratios like the intervals of just intonation (Doc.~060); the two exceptions, the primes $13$
  and $7$, are in the Galois reading the harmonic primes of depth $k=3$ and $k=6$, and all nine
  lie in the grid $\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}$ (Doc.~358, Theorem~B \BM).
\item \textbf{The mass ladder is a logarithmic resonance scale.} Because $\xi\ll1$, the exponent
  axis is a musical scale: a step $\Delta p=1/3$ is the fixed interval $\xi^{1/3}\approx1/19.6$,
  the ``third'' of the particle spectrum (Doc.~189).
\item \textbf{The comma is the embedding error.} Euler's spiral of fifths and thirds never
  closes, because $2^x3^y5^z=1$ holds only for $x=y=z=0$; commas remain \QM. In a finite field,
  by contrast, every circle of fifths closes exactly, and a comma is impossible there (Doc.~358,
  Theorem~A \BM). The musical comma and the fractal correction are therefore not a property of
  the Galois structure but of the embedding into the continuum; this is why $K_{\text{frak}}$
  appears only in the SI translation. The $\xi$ cycle itself is defined rolled up, with the
  rational step $1/75$, and closes after exactly 75 turns (Doc.~315 \BM); the rotation number
  $74/75$ is the same value as $K_{\text{frak}}$.
\end{itemize}
The same structure recurs in coupling: undercritical, critical and overcritical coupling of two
circuits correspond exactly to the $2\times2$ mixing of two states (Doc.~328 \BM); consonance is
coupling geometry. The limits are recorded just as clearly: the harmonic reading is a
representation, not an additional input; a Tonnetz with a finite limit covers only finitely many
primes (Doc.~316), and hit rates of harmonic assignments with wide tolerance windows are a
coverage effect, not evidence (Doc.~343).

\subsection{Guidelines}

Five rules determine how results are to be read:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Ratios are what counts.} An absolute value in SI tests the conversion, not the
  structure (A130).
\item \textbf{FFGFT works with $\xi$ alone.} Measured values from SI and the Standard Model serve
  only for conversion and comparison, and for that exactly one measured value is needed as anchor
  (Doc.~383).
\item \textbf{Integer structural choices are choices.} $\mathbb{Z}_3$, $D_4$, the winding numbers
  or the exponent~10 in the cosmic sector are motivated choices, not calibrations.
\item \textbf{The cosmic sector is set aside.} $H_0$ and $\Lambda$ are deliberately excluded from
  the derivation of constants (P39): the Standard Model does not derive them either, and none of
  the frameworks compared determines the ratio of Planck length to Hubble length from first
  principles.
\item \textbf{Explanation before precision.} What FFGFT yields are explanations: why masses stand
  in fixed ratios, why charge is quantised, why there are three generations, why Koide's $Q=2/3$
  holds, why the Born rule is a geometric quotient. The numbers test these explanations; they do
  not replace the calculations of the Standard Model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the corpus no longer claims}

The calculation review has withdrawn several earlier claims:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item FFGFT is not a precision extension of the Standard Model. Where the Standard Model
  calculates, FFGFT gives the same values or uses the measured ones as anchors; a measurable $\xi$
  correction that would be more precise does not exist (R142, Doc.~382). Where it has free
  parameters, FFGFT supplies structure at the per-cent to per-mille level, not at measurement
  precision.
\item FFGFT is not ``postulate-free'': $\tilde T\cdot m=1$, $T^4$, $\mathbb{Z}_3$, $D_4$ and $\xi$
  are chosen.
\item Precision tables of older documents in which prefactors had been fitted to measured values
  are not evidence (R138).
\item The agreement of the SI value of $\alpha$ is a property of the chosen anchor, not an
  independent prediction (R135).
\item The precision of $G$ to $0.003\,\%$ is not evidence in its own right (R141).
\item A $\xi$ effect in Bell experiments or quantum gates that is measurable with present-day
  hardware does not exist (R142, R143).
\end{itemize}
Where such statements appear in older documents, they carry a dated note.

\textbf{Shor in the deterministic reading.} Shor's algorithm splits into the preparation, a
modular exponentiation with $O(n^3)$ gates and almost the entire cost (share $1-1/(n+1)$, $99.95\,\%$ for
2048~bits \KM), and the quantum Fourier
transform with $O(n^2)$ gates, a wave operation that extracts the period as a resonance
(Doc.~176). In theory it factorises in $O((\log N)^3)$ \KM; a general exponential quantum
advantage, however, is not supported (Doc.~176). The advantage lies solely in the period finding.
Preparation and error correction cost more on quantum hardware than classically: the preparation
by a fixed factor, the error correction by an overhead that, according to the threshold theorem,
grows only slowly. The advantage would take effect only for numbers for which the hardware is
lacking today (RSA-2048: $8.6\times10^9$ gates, $4.1\times10^6$ qubits \KM); in the reachable range a
classical computer factorises just as fast. As an apparatus the quantum computer is an analogue
computer with quantum-mechanical precision: a qubit is a two-level resonator, every gate a
resonant drive, and error correction averages over syndrome rounds. This averaging runs into the
same resolution floor as every evaluation; that it falls below it at scale is what the threshold
theorem claims, but it has not been shown \SM. Whether a net advantage remains is thus decided by
the error correction alone. If $\xi$ is the floor, the limit of a resonance-based computer lies
at $N_\mathrm{max}\approx1/\xi=7500$, about 13~bits, and the time advantage vanishes structurally
\SM\ (Doc.~343, Theorem~G$'$).
There are alternatives for the computational operations themselves: the Fourier transform runs
optically or in any resonator (Doc.~176), analogue matrix folding and resonator locking compute
with resonances instead of gates (Doc.~186), as do resonance computers such as the BAW Ising
machine (Doc.~362). They are subject to the same resolution floor. The calculations of standard
quantum mechanics remain usable; FFGFT interprets them as resonance processes. What is withdrawn
is only the earlier claim that a separate T0 or $\xi$ factorisation method yields results
distinguishable from Shor's (R65); $\xi$ as a line width of phase estimation is refuted for the
preregistered setup, the line remains open (P44).
