\section*{Abstract}

In \enquote{Black Holes from Hyperbits} (IPI 2026) Douglas Matzke derives
the entire physics of black holes from the Clifford-algebra axiom
$e_i^2 = +1$, without general relativity and without free parameters.
Central to this: the mass of a bit follows from Landauer's principle
($E_{\mathrm{bit}} = k_B T_P \ln 2$) plus $E = mc^2$ and is therefore a
\emph{universal} number — $m_{\mathrm{bit}} = m_P \ln 2/(2\pi) \approx 0.11\,m_P$,
independent of the physical system.

FFGFT (Fundamental Fractal Geometric Field Theory) arrives at a
fundamentally different insight: bit values are \emph{system-dependent}.
The minimal information unit is the winding quantum $\Delta w = 1$ on $T^4$
(Doc.\ 257), and its energy is $E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \hbar c/L$ — dependent on
the physical scale $L$ of the system. Landauer's $k_B T \ln 2$ is the
special case of one particular scale and temperature, not a universal law
(A271, A272, A273, Doc.\ 302).

This document compares both frameworks and works out the difference in
the Landauer interpretation as theoretically essential.

\section{The Landauer question: universal vs.\ system-dependent bit values}

This is the theoretically deepest difference between the two frameworks.

\subsection{Matzke's position: a universal bit value}

Matzke fixes the Landauer energy scale at the Planck temperature $T_P$:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{bit}} = k_B\,T_P\,\ln 2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  m_{\mathrm{bit}} = \frac{m_P\,\ln 2}{2\pi} \approx 0.110\,m_P
  \tag{Matzke, Ch.\ 6}
\end{equation}
This value is \emph{universal}: every bit carries the same mass,
independent of the system (electron, black hole, vacuum).
From it follow the stability threshold
$n_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 2\pi/(\sqrt{2}\ln 2) = 6.41$ and the
Schwarzschild relation directly.

\subsection{FFGFT position: system-dependent bit values}

FFGFT understands Landauer's principle as a statement about
\emph{phase-space regions} of a thermal carrier ensemble, not about
abstract information (A271):

\begin{quote}
  \itshape
  Counting proceeds by distinguishability (orthogonality), not by
  energy. Thermodynamics counts regions and does not read them —
  there is no thermodynamic truth detector.
  Sufficient for heat production is the \textbf{non-bijectivity of the
  region map}, not logical irreversibility.
\end{quote}

\medskip
The fundamental bit energy in FFGFT is (Docs.\ 257, 302):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \frac{\hbar c}{L}
  \tag{FFGFT, Doc.\ 257}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the physical scale of the system.
Landauer's $k_B T\ln 2$ is the limiting case at one particular scale
and bath temperature (A273: \enquote{two floors — the thermal $k_BT$
and the quantum-geometric $\hbar c/L$; the larger one governs}).

\subsection{Two floors, one principle}

FFGFT distinguishes (A273, Doc.\ 290):

\begin{description}
  \item[Static] Bit $\equiv$ winding quantum $\Delta w = 1$ on $T^4$:
    scale-universal, energy-independent as a counting quantity;
    the energy $E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \hbar c/L$ is scale-specific.
  \item[Thermodynamic] Irreversible erasure:
    $Q \geq k_B T\ln 2$ — cost in joules, dependent on the bath
    temperature $T$. This price has nothing to do with the
    information-theoretic quantity (A272: \enquote{no energy can be
    assigned to an information bit, only to its carrier}).
  \item[Kinematic] Reversible bit-flip time:
    $\widetilde{T} = \hbar/E_{\mathrm{bit}}$;
    Margolus--Levitin $\tau \geq \pi\hbar/(2E)$;
    energy sets the clock without loss (Doc.\ 290).
\end{description}

The four guises of a bit energy at the characteristic scale $E_0 = 7.35$ MeV:
\begin{equation}
  m_0 c^2 = E_0 = k_B T_0 = \hbar/\widetilde{T}
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  T_0 = E_0/k_B \approx \num{8.5e10}\,\mathrm{K}
\end{equation}
Landauer at room temperature gives $Q \approx \num{3e-21}$ J $\approx 18$ meV —
eight orders of magnitude below $E_0$.
The statements \enquote{what the bit is} ($E_0 = 7.35$ MeV)
and \enquote{what erasure costs} ($k_BT\ln 2 = 18$ meV at 300 K)
belong to different layers (Doc.\ 290, A271).

\section{FFGFT objections to Matzke's Landauer bridge}

Three concrete objections from the FFGFT corpus:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Landauer applies to carrier operations, not to abstract
  information} (A272, A273).
  What Landauer 1961 proves is a lower bound for \emph{carrier
  operations on a thermal equilibrium ensemble} — not a universal
  statement about abstract information.
  The identification $m_{\mathrm{bit}} = m_P\ln 2/(2\pi)$ treats the
  bit as a thermodynamic object at Planck temperature.
  That is a realization assumption, not a law.

\item \textbf{The bit energy is scale-specific} (Docs.\ 302, 257).
  In FFGFT the minimal information unit is the winding quantum
  $\Delta w = 1$ on $T^4$ — scale-universal as a \emph{counting
  quantity}, but with scale-specific energy
  $E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \hbar c/L$.
  Landauer's $k_BT\ln 2$ is the special case at one particular scale
  and bath temperature. The phrase \enquote{the bit energy is
  $m_P\ln 2/(2\pi)$} implies a scale that is not distinguished in FFGFT.

\item \textbf{The stability threshold is not a purely algebraic
  statement} (derived from points 1 and 2).
  $n_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 2\pi/(\sqrt{2}\ln 2) = 6.41$ follows from the
  Compton--Schwarzschild equation plus
  $m_{\mathrm{bit}} = m_P\ln 2/(2\pi)$.
  The first piece (Compton $=$ Schwarzschild) is geometric \B.
  The second (Landauer at $T_P$) is a postulate.
  The apparent elegance of the result ($\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ exactly 0.41
  bits below threshold) hinges on this postulate.
  A system-dependent bit energy would shift the threshold.

\end{enumerate}

\section*{Conclusion}

Matzke's hyperbit framework and FFGFT are complementary structures with
remarkably similar structural findings: fermion generation = algebraic
base unit, $N_c = 3$ enforced, Schwarzschild radius without GR.

The deepest difference lies in the treatment of bit values.
Matzke posits a universal bit value via the Landauer bridge at Planck
temperature — an elegant but, by FFGFT standards, inadmissible
universalization. FFGFT holds: bit values are system-dependent
($E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \hbar c/L$), Landauer applies to carrier
operations of a thermal ensemble, and the stability threshold
$n_{\mathrm{thresh}}$ would then not be a universal number.

This is no objection to the algebraic beauty of Matzke's approach,
but a theoretical difference in foundations.
Doc.\ 325 makes it constructive: the complete Hawking physics —
temperature, selection, carrier, information conservation — follows
from $\widetilde{T}\cdot m = 1$, the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ structure and the
system-dependent bit energy, without a universal bit value.
Matzke's $n_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 6.41$ thus demonstrably hinges on the
bit-value parametrization, not on the temperature physics.

\begin{tcolorbox}[ffgftbox, title={Main finding}]
  Matzke and FFGFT converge on \textbf{fermion generation = fundamental
  algebraic unit}. They diverge on \textbf{bit values}: universal
  (Matzke, Landauer at $T_P$) vs.\ system-dependent (FFGFT,
  $E_{\mathrm{bit}} = \hbar c/L$).
  This difference is not cosmetic — it concerns the stability threshold
  and the mass of a bit. The Hawking temperature formula itself is
  untouched by it: both routes give the same number, but only the FFGFT
  route (Doc.\ 325) does without a universal bit value.
\end{tcolorbox}
