\section*{Abstract}

This document compares the results of the Galois documents 338--344 systematically
with the established literature on algebraic derivations of the Standard Model
(division algebras, Clifford algebras, exceptional Jordan algebras) and on neutrino
physics (seesaw mechanism, $0\nu\beta\beta$). It names where FFGFT achieves something
absent from the literature, and where the literature is ahead of FFGFT. The criterion
is not ``is the algebra elegant'' but ``does the algebra give testable numbers''.

\textbf{References.} All citations were verified on 4~September 2026 against arXiv,
INSPIRE, APS and publisher pages.

\section{What FFGFT achieves beyond the literature}

\subsection{Prime structure algebraically forced \KM}
\textbf{Literature.} The harmonic primes $\{5,7,11,13\}$ appear in music theory
(Partch, just intonation) and phenomenological mass formulae (Koide 1983, Brannen 2006)
as observed, never derived. Furey and Dixon work in characteristic 0; GF$(3^k)$
does not appear in any comparison approach.
\textbf{FFGFT.} Docs.~342/343: these primes are the only ones entering
$|\mathrm{GF}(3^k)^*|$ at $k=3,4,5,6$; $p=13$ is the worldwide unique prime with
$\mathrm{ord}_p(3)=3$. A theorem, not a fit.
\textbf{Assessment.} New in this form.

\subsection{Coupling constant not a free parameter \KM}
\textbf{Literature.} In the SM all Yukawa couplings are free (13 parameters in the
fermion sector). Furey 2015 derives gauge groups, not couplings. Koide 1983 finds
$Q=2/3$ but cannot explain why.
\textbf{FFGFT.} Doc.~338: $\xi=(r_\mu/r_e)^2/43200$; hence $m_\mu/m_e=120\sqrt3$
(0.52\,\%) and $1/\alpha=3700/27$ (7.6 ppm).
\textbf{Assessment.} Concrete numerical progress beyond all comparison approaches.
Caveat: the torus quantum numbers $r_i,p_i$ are geometrically motivated (Doc.~006),
not yet algebraically forced.

\subsection{Neutrino--vacuum identity \BM}
\textbf{Literature.} Furey 2015 constructs the neutrino as the Fock vacuum of Cl$(6)$ ---
known. \textbf{FFGFT.} Doc.~344: the same follows independently from GF$(27)^*$:
$\nu_1$ in $O_1=\{1,3,9\}$, the generator orbit. Two unrelated frameworks place
the same particle at the same algebraic position.
\textbf{Assessment.} The convergence is new.

\subsection{Closure of the classification: no fourth generation \BM}
\textbf{Literature.} Furey 2018 and Todorov/Dubois-Violette 2018 show ``there are three'',
not ``there cannot be four'' --- a construction, not a theorem.
\textbf{FFGFT.} $\varphi(26)=12\to$ exactly 4 orbits; sector pairing without fixed point
$\to$ exactly 2 layers; orbit length $3\to$ exactly 3 generations. A counting argument
with completeness.
\textbf{Assessment.} The completeness conclusion is new in this sharpness.

\subsection{Falsifiable numerical predictions \KM/\BM}
\textbf{Literature.} Furey, Dixon, Günaydin give structural statements, no mass values.
The seesaw (Minkowski 1977, Mohapatra/Senjanović 1979, Weinberg 1979) explains the
smallness of neutrino mass but predicts no number --- $M_R$ is free.
\textbf{FFGFT.} Doc.~340: $m_{\nu_1}=4.54$, $m_{\nu_2}=9.81$, $m_{\nu_3}=49.9$~meV;
$\Delta m^2$ to 0.5--1\,\%. Doc.~344: $m_{ee}=0$ (Dirac) or $\approx7.1$~meV (Majorana) ---
nEXO decides.
\textbf{Assessment.} The corpus makes concrete, falsifiable predictions no algebraic
comparison approach provides. This is the main difference.

\subsection{Gravity as geometry, not force \BM}
Furey and Dixon do not treat gravity;
their algebras yield the SM gauge groups, not spacetime. In FFGFT gravity is
\emph{not a separate force}: $G=\xi^2/(4\,m_\text{char})$ follows intrinsically
from the $T^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$ torus geometry (Doc.~003, register R58). No separate
quantum gravity is required since the geometry is pre-ordered, not a dynamical
field to be quantised. The time field $T(x)$ with $T\cdot m=1$ replaces metric
coupling. Caveat: the Galois structure GF$(3^k)$ has not yet been applied to $G$
itself --- the derivation runs via $\xi$ and torus geometry, not group orders.


\section{What the literature achieves beyond FFGFT}

\textbf{Gauge groups directly from the algebra.} Furey 2015: SU(3)$\times$U(1) from
Cl$(6)$ commutators with correct charges for all 8 fermions. Dixon 1994: the full
SU(3)$\times$SU(2)$\times$U(1). FFGFT has this only sketched (Doc.~339).
\textbf{Literature ahead.}

\textbf{Quark sector.} Furey/Dixon treat quarks on equal footing. FFGFT has quark masses
(Doc.~006) but no Galois orbit assignment for quarks. \textbf{Literature ahead.}

\textbf{Seesaw mechanism.} Explains \emph{why} neutrinos are light and provides a
dynamical mechanism for Majorana masses. FFGFT replaces this by double-$\xi$ suppression
(Doc.~007) --- shorter, but without dynamics. \textbf{Literature mechanistically ahead.}

\textbf{Rigour.} Furey 2015 is a thesis with complete proofs. The FFGFT corpus uses
verification scripts and numerical checks (\BM), not complete analytic proofs for all
theorems. \textbf{Literature formally stricter.}

\section{The central difference}

\begin{quote}
Furey, Dixon, Günaydin show: \emph{the algebra has the right shape.}\\
FFGFT shows: \emph{the algebra gives the right numbers} ---
$m_{\nu_1}=4.54$~meV, $\Delta m^2$ to 1\,\%, $1/\alpha$ to 7.6~ppm, $m_\mu/m_e$ to 0.5\,\%.
\end{quote}

Not a contradiction but a complement. Furey provides the gauge structure, the FFGFT
Galois structure provides the mass scale. Doc.~344 shows both converge at Su[0] $=$ Vss[0].
A unification --- Cl$(6)$ over GF$(9)$ with GF$(27)^*$ orbit structure --- is the
natural next task.
