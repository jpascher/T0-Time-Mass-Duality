\begin{abstract}
Two independently developed frameworks — the Fundamental Fractal Geometric
Field Theory (FFGFT) with its compact T$^4/\mathbb{Z}_3$ geometry and
Matzke's GALG with ternary algebra $\mathbb{Z}_3\mathbb{C}$ — arrive at
the same $\mathbb{Z}_3$ structure by entirely different routes.
This document shows algebraically \emph{why} this is not a coincidence,
and derives new physical relations from it.

The key is the \emph{Frobenius automorphism} $x\mapsto x^3$ in GF(9):
it simultaneously realises the FFGFT sector pairing $k\leftrightarrow -k$,
Matzke's vacuum conjugation $V_s=V^\dagger$, and the field conjugation
in $\mathbb{Z}_3\mathbb{C}$ \BM.

The main new results:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Wolfenstein parameter of the CKM matrix satisfies
    $\lambda = \xi^{1/6}$ (0.5\% deviation), where the exponent $1/6$
    follows directly from the Galois tower structure
    $\mathrm{GF}(3)\subset\mathrm{GF}(9)\subset\mathrm{GF}(27)$ \KM.
  \item The CKM element $|V_{ub}| = \sqrt{\xi}/3 = \sqrt{\xi}\cdot(C_2-1)$
    (0.8\% deviation), where $C_2=4/3$ is the Casimir value of SU(3) \KM.
  \item GF(9) and GF(27) classify the particle spectrum topologically —
    but exact mass values require $\xi\in\mathbb{R}$ \BM.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Notation:} Symmetric $\mathbb{Z}_3\mathbb{C}$ after Matzke:
$a+bi$ with $a,b\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, $+1+1=-1$ (ternary inline carry).
\end{abstract}

\section*{Guide: what this document shows}

\textbf{The basic problem.}
The three quark generations mix with strongly different strengths.
The Wolfenstein parameter $\lambda\approx 0.225$ describes this hierarchy
empirically — but why exactly this value? Why three generations?
Why do the muon and electron have such different masses, even though
both are leptons?

\textbf{What GF(9) and GF(27) contribute.}
The finite field GF(9) has exactly 9 elements. Its Frobenius automorphism
$x\mapsto x^3$ partitions them into three classes: fixed points $\{0,+1,-1\}$
(the FFGFT sectors), and three conjugate pairs $(k,-k)$ (the sector pairing).
GF(27) extends this: its Galois tower over GF(3) has degree~6,
and the 8 three-cycles correspond to 8 quark-colour states in
3 generations.

\textbf{What $\xi$ does that GF(9) cannot.}
GF(9) is a finite field — it has exactly 9 elements,
and none of them equals $m_e/m_P \approx 4\times 10^{-23}$.
Exact particle masses cannot be represented in a finite field.
The transition to physical scales occurs through $\xi = 4/30\,000$,
whose denominator~3 forms a singularity in GF(3) — this is algebraically
the transition from discrete topology to continuous physics.

\textbf{The surprising part.}
Despite this, the combination of GF tower structure and $\xi$ yields precise
numerical predictions for CKM parameters that were previously known only
empirically. This suggests that the Wolfenstein hierarchy is not a free
parametrisation but follows from algebraic structure.

\section{Motivation: Characteristic~3 and FFGFT}

In characteristic~3, $(1+N)^3 = 1$ for nilpotent $N$ with $N^2=0$
holds \emph{algebraically exactly} — because $\binom{3}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{3}$
for $0<k<3$. The machine-epsilon result
$|T_k^3-\mathbf{1}|<6.5\times10^{-16}$ from Doc.~324 is therefore not an
approximation but a consequence of an algebraic theorem~\BM.

\subsection{Question 1: Number operator $N$ and FFGFT winding quantum $\Delta w$}

\begin{equation}
  N\bmod 3 = \{0,+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1,0\}

\begin{align*}
  N\bmod 3 = 0&: \text{ colour-neutral (neutrino, positron)} \\
  N\bmod 3 = 1&: \text{ anti-down quarks (3 colours)} \\
