\section{Liouville and the Region}

The Liouville theorem: The phase space volume of a closed system
is invariant. When a region mapping $f: \{A, B\} \to Z$ reduces several
initial regions to one target region, the "missing" volume must go
somewhere. The bath takes it over. \status{B}

That is the physical core of the Landauer limit --- without semantics,
without the concept of information.

\section{Three Region Operations}

\textbf{Bijective mapping:} $W_{\text{after}} = W_{\text{before}}$.
No costs. Transistor flips from A to B --- a bijective operation.
No volume loss. \status{B}

\textbf{Non-bijective mapping:} $W_{\text{after}} < W_{\text{before}}$.
Minimum costs: $Q \ge k_BT \ln(W_{\text{before}}/W_{\text{after}})$.
RESTORE TO ONE --- the classical bit erasure. \status{B}

\textbf{Decoherence:} The superposition region $\{|\psi\rangle\}$ is
reduced to a classical region. That is also a non-bijective
region mapping. The costs fall to the bath --- exactly Landauer. \status{B}

\begin{laierspur}
Bijective is like rearranging furniture --- same objects,
different arrangement, nothing lost. Non-bijective is like pushing furniture
together --- a corner of the room remains free, and the room
has not gained more space: The freed part
must reappear somewhere else.
\end{laierspur}

\section{The Reversal Test}

Doc.\,301 formulates the \textbf{reversal test} as an operational criterion:
An operation is exactly then subject to Landauer costs when its reversal
is \emph{physically} not feasible --- without involving a bath.

That is sharper than the usual formulation: Not the logical
irreversibility (Landauer 1961) decides, but the physical
non-bijectivity of the region mapping. \status{B Doc.\,301}

\section{Unit Cells and Spectral Response}

When the phase space is divided into unit cells $h^f$, each
cell gives exactly one orthogonal answer. The spectral structure of the
Landauer bookkeeping is thus completely determined by $h^f$ and the ratio
$W_{\text{before}}/W_{\text{after}}$ --- no free parameters.
\status{B Doc.\,302}
