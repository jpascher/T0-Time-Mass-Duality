\section{The Central Formula}

\textbf{[K]} In this theory the gravitational constant is not an independent
quantity but follows from $\xi$ and a mass:
\[
  G = \frac{\xi^2}{4\,m_\text{char}} \qquad\text{(natural units)}.
\]

This is a \emph{derivation}, not a bridge. G is not absorbed as a unit factor
(that would be A080), but expressed as a function of the single parameter and
a characteristic mass. The difference from A130 is decisive: there $\alpha$
was in the end calibrated; here the \emph{structure} of G is determined from $\xi$.

\textbf{[B]} The equivalent form
\[
  \xi = 2\sqrt{G\,m_\text{char}}
\]
shows the statement from the other side: $\xi$, the tetrahedral packing quantity
from A020, \emph{is} up to the factor the square root of the product of
gravitational strength and characteristic mass. Gravity and geometry are thereby
not two things but two readings of $\xi$.

This is a statement about structure only in natural units (A085). In SI it would
be an equation between incommensurable units.

\textbf{[K]} For context: in natural units $G = 1$, exactly as
$c = \hbar = k_B = \alpha = 1$ (A085, A130). The SI value $6{.}674\cdot10^{-11}$
is bookkeeping. The formula $G = \xi^2/(4m)$ therefore does not say what native
value $G$ has -- that is one -- but how the \emph{SI dress} of $G$ composes from
$\xi$ and a mass. This is the same bookkeeping as for $\alpha$, only for a
dimensioned rather than a dimensionless quantity.

\section{Why This Is a Particularly Direct Derivation}

\textbf{[K]} As with $\alpha$ (A130), there is no circularity at the core
of the formula here either -- for $\alpha$ the initial circularity is broken by
the two-route over-determination of $E_0$; for G it is absent from the outset:
$G = \xi^2/(4m)$ does not use G anywhere to determine G.
The only difference is directness -- $\alpha$ needs the meeting of two routes,
G does not because the formula simply does not contain G. A hidden calibration
is not in the core here either, only in the SI dress ($E_\text{char}$, see below).
The formula says: choose the characteristic mass -- say $m_e$ -- and the
gravitational strength follows from $\xi$.

\textbf{[B]} This is the actual resolution of the gravity problem in this theory,
and it is stronger than what A140 states. A140 says: the QG problem does not arise
because the geometry is pre-ordered. That is a \emph{negative} argument. A145 says:
G has a \emph{positive} expression from $\xi$. The negative argument explains why
quantisation is unnecessary; the positive explains where the numerical value comes
from. This makes G the most direct $\xi$-manifestation in the corpus -- not the
most precisely tested (that is the lepton ladder A110 and the $\alpha$ over-determination
A130), but the most immediate in form.

\section{The Dimension Bridge $E_\text{char}$}

\textbf{[K]} The basic formula $G=\xi^2/(4m)$ is not yet dimensionally complete:
in natural units $G$ has dimension $[E^{-2}]$, but the right-hand side has $[E^{-1}]$.
A factor $1/E_\text{char}$ is missing:
\[
  G_\text{nat} = \frac{\xi_0^2}{4\,m_e}\cdot\frac{1}{E_\text{char}},
  \qquad E_\text{char}\approx 28{.}4 .
\]

\textbf{[K]} The corpus provides a structural decomposition of $E_\text{char}$:
\[
  E_\text{char} = E_0\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac43\Bigr)^2\cdot\frac{\pi}{\sqrt2}\cdot
  K_\text{frak} = 7{.}40\cdot 1{.}778\cdot 2{.}221\cdot 0{.}986 = 28{.}8 ,
\]
against the target value $28{.}4$, i.e.\ $+1{.}5\,\%$.

\textbf{The assessment -- and it is reassuring.} $E_\text{char}$ carries two quantities
that are not purely geometric elsewhere: $E_0$ (A130) and $K_\text{frak}$ (A040).
The factor $(4/3)^2$ is geometric, $\pi/\sqrt2$ likewise; $E_0$ and $K_\text{frak}$
are not. But this concerns only the \emph{SI numerical value} of $G$ -- and that is
bookkeeping. How precisely $E_\text{char}$ hits $28{.}4$ is a statement about the
scale conversion, not about the structure. As long as one works with ratios, the
measured value of $G$ is irrelevant; the structural relations are known and confirmed
in SI and in the Standard Model. The \emph{basic structure} $G = \xi^2/(4m)$ is
unaffected by this and is the actual statement.

Cleanly separated: the statement ``G is proportional to $\xi^2$ and inversely
proportional to a mass'' is \textbf{[K]}. The precise numerical value via
$E_\text{char}$ is \textbf{[B]} with a calibrated component.

\section{The Same Diagnosis as for $\alpha$, More Complex Dress}

\textbf{[K]} G passes the same test by which A135 decides whether a quantity is
fundamental: \emph{can it be set to one by a consistent unit choice?} For G the
answer is yes -- in Planck units $G = c = \hbar = 1$. By the A135 diagnosis this
means: G is not content of the theory but a conversion factor. It thereby stands
on the same side as $c$, $\hbar$, $k_B$ (physically one via duality) and $\alpha$
(one via normalisation) -- all convention. The SI value $6{.}674\cdot10^{-11}$ is
what this convention conceals, not a fundamental numerical value.

\textbf{[K]} And as with $\alpha$, the content lies not in this value but in $\xi$.
The bare coin is $\xi$; G is -- seen in SI light -- the same coin, only dressed.
That is the meaning of $G = \xi^2/(4m_\text{char})$: not a second degree of freedom
but $\xi$ in dimensioned form. Equivalent information content, not equal rank --
exactly the resolution of the apparent tension from A135.

\textbf{[K]} The difference from $\alpha$ is only the \emph{richness of the dress},
and it has a dimensional reason. In natural units $\alpha$ is dimensionless, $[E^0]$;
its SI form therefore needs exactly one scale:
\[
  \alpha_\text{SI} = \xi\cdot E_0^2 \qquad\text{(one scale, squared).}
\]
G by contrast has dimension $[E^{-2}]$ in natural units (it is $M_\text{Planck}^{-2}$)
and thereby ties mass to length to time. Its SI form can therefore not make do
with one scale; it needs a mass \emph{and} the intermediate scale $E_\text{char}$:
\[
  G_\text{nat} = \frac{\xi^2}{4\,m_\text{char}}\cdot\frac{1}{E_\text{char}}.
\]
$E_\text{char}$ is itself multi-layered -- $E_0\cdot(4/3)^2\cdot(\pi/\sqrt2)\cdot
K_\text{frak}$ (see above) -- whereas $\alpha$ makes do with the one $E_0$.
This is the ``more complex SI dress'': not more content but more layers of the
same content, forced by the richer dimension of G.

\section{What Counts: Exact Reconstructibility}

\textbf{[K]} The central statement of this document is not how precisely the
$0{.}5\,\%$ is hit, but that G in natural units can be set to one -- and from this
something exact follows. If G is a convention (one in Planck units), then its SI
value is not an independent numerical value but \emph{completely} fixed by the
conversion:
\[
  G_\text{SI} = \frac{\hbar c}{M_\text{Planck}^2} .
\]
Given $\xi$ and one absolute scale, $G_\text{SI}$ is thereby \emph{exactly}
determined -- without a free parameter, without approximation. This is the same
structure as $\alpha_\text{SI} = \xi\,E_0^2$: the dimensioned constant is the
SI expression of $\xi$ plus scale, and the reconstruction is exact, not approximate.

\textbf{[K]} The $E_\text{char}$ decomposition is strictly separate from this.
The exact $E_\text{char}$ -- the one that hits $G_\text{SI}$ -- is already fixed
by $\xi$ and the scale. What is $0{.}5\,\%$ off is not this exact quantity but
the \emph{attempt to express it by a closed formula} from known quantities
($E_0\cdot(4/3)^2\cdot(\pi/\sqrt2)\cdot K_\text{frak}$). The deviation measures
the quality of this \emph{analytical approximation of the dress}, not the
reconstructibility itself. The reconstruction is exact; only its shortcut to
a simple expression is not yet.

\textbf{[STIPULATION]} The priority is thereby correct: what distinguishes G from
a genuinely fundamental constant is the settability to one and the resulting exact
reconstructibility -- and that is shown. The remaining $E_\text{char}$ approximation
is a question of closed form, not of structure, and carries no weight for the
central statement.

\section{Open Questions}

\textbf{First, the characteristic mass is not uniquely fixed.} The corpus uses
$m_e$; why the electron and not another mass is the characteristic one is not
justified.

\textbf{Second, $E_\text{char}$ carries a calibrated component} (see above).
The basic structure $\xi^2/(4m)$ is unaffected by this; the precise numerical
value is not. \emph{This residual is explicitly not a measurement effect.} It is
tempting to attribute the $\sim0{.}5\,\%$ to the notorious measurement weakness
of G -- but the opposite is the case: G is measured to $2\cdot10^{-5}$ (about
$0{.}002\,\%$), and even the large scatter between individual experiments only
reaches $\sim0{.}05\,\%$. The theory's residual is thus roughly $200$ times larger
than the measurement uncertainty and about $10$ times larger than the experimental
scatter; the inputs to the dress ($E_0$ via masses to $\sim10^{-8}$, $K_\text{frak}$
derived, the geometry factors exact) do not carry it either. The residual is thus
structural -- a residual of the $E_\text{char}$ approximation -- and must remain
named as such; attributing it to measurement imprecision would be the same
target-first assignment that A105 and A130 warn against.

\textbf{Third, the residual is entangled with a Standard Model assumption --
the mass definition.} \emph{Measured} lepton masses enter the dress
($E_0=\sqrt{m_e m_\mu}$, $m_\text{char}=m_e$), and ``mass'' in the Standard
Model is scheme-dependent: the measured values are \emph{pole masses}, whereas a
purely geometric construction could equally correspond to a running (MS-bar) mass.
The difference between both schemes is of order $\alpha/\pi \approx 0{.}23\,\%$
per mass; since G depends on the masses via $G\sim 1/(m_e^{3/2} m_\mu^{1/2})$
with total mass power $2$, this propagates to $\sim 0{.}46\,\%$ on G -- \emph{the
same order as the residual}. This cuts both ways: it shows that the $0{.}5\,\%$
\emph{cannot} be cleanly read as a geometric defect because part of it may be a
convention on the SM input side; but it does \emph{not} show that the scheme
effect closes the gap. That would only be shown by computing the pole-to-running
shift for precisely these masses and this formula with sign and coefficient
\emph{forward} -- an order-of-magnitude argument is not a derivation. Only this
remains: the residual lies between geometric approximation and mass scheme, and
both are of the same order.

\textbf{Fourth, the gravitational dynamics are still missing.} A145 gives the
\emph{value} of G from $\xi$; it does not give the field equations from which
the inverse-square law and motion would follow. This remains the large open point
already named in A140.
