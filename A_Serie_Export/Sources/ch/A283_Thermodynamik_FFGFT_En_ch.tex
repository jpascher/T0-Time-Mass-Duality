\section{Level 2 -- Landauer as phase-space accounting (A271)}

\subsection{The phase-space formulation}

\textbf{[SET]} A physical system occupies a region in phase space
of size $W$ (measured in $h^f$). The region exists independently
of how one labels it.

\textbf{[B]} A mapping that reduces multiple input regions $\{A, B\}$
to a single target region $Z$ reduces the volume:
$\Delta S_\text{system} = -k_B \ln(W_{A+B}/W_Z) \le 0$.
Liouville's theorem forbids volume shrinkage in a closed system.
A heat bath must absorb the volume:
\[
\boxed{Q \ge k_B\,T\,\ln\!\left(\frac{W_\text{before}}{W_\text{after}}\right)}
\]
with $\ln 2$ as the binary special case.

\subsection{Three separations}

\textbf{[B]}
(1) \emph{Bit $\ne$ physical region.} Shannon and Boltzmann entropy
are identical only under uniform distribution over physical microstates.
(2) \emph{Content neutrality.} Thermodynamics counts regions, does not
read them. Bit "true" and bit "false" cost identically.
(3) \emph{Three levels separated.} Construction / state transition / description.
Landauer costs occur at levels~1 and~2, not at~3.

\section{Minimal length and fractal refinement: an apparent contradiction}

\textbf{[S]} At first glance, there appears to be a tension:
On the one hand, FFGFT speaks of a \emph{minimal length} at the antipode
($\bar\lambda = \hbar/(m_\text{min} c)$), on the other hand, of a
\emph{fractal refinement inward} along the time cycle.
Is this a contradiction?

The answer is \textbf{no} -- the key lies in the \emph{reference-frame
	logic} of the theory.

\subsection{What does "minimal length" mean?}

The "minimal length" in FFGFT is \emph{not} an absolute Planck length,
but a \textbf{mass-dependent quantity}:

\[
\bar\lambda = \frac{\hbar}{m c}, \qquad
a_0 \propto \frac{1}{m_e}, \qquad
\tilde{T} = \frac{1}{m}.
\]

At the antipode ($m \to m_\text{min}$), these lengths become \emph{maximal}.
There, massive structures are least sharply resolved --
a regime of \emph{minimal metric sharpness}, dominated by radiation.
The "minimal length" is thus actually the \emph{largest characteristic
	length} of the system.

\subsection{What does "fractal refinement inward" mean?}

With increasing mass $m(\theta)$ along the time cycle, all
characteristic lengths become \emph{shorter}:

\[
\bar\lambda \propto \frac{1}{m(\theta)}, \qquad
a_0 \propto \frac{1}{m_e(\theta)}.
\]

This means: matter becomes \emph{more differentiated}, \emph{more structured},
\emph{more finely resolved} -- like a \textbf{zoom inward}.
Space itself does not expand; the \emph{scale of objects} changes.

\subsection{Why is this not a contradiction?}

The apparent tension dissolves once one distinguishes two
\emph{different perspectives}:

\begin{center}
	\small
	\begin{tabular}{p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.48\textwidth}}
		\toprule
		\textbf{Perspective} & \textbf{Meaning in FFGFT} \\
		\midrule
		Minimal length (at the antipode) & 
		Maximal Compton wavelength, minimal structuring,
		radiation-dominated regime \\
		Fractal refinement inward & 
		Increasing differentiation, growing masses,
		shorter wavelengths, present matter domination \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \textbf{direction} is unambiguous:

\[
\text{Antipode} \;(m_\text{min},\; \bar\lambda_\text{max})
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{present position} \;(m_0,\; \bar\lambda_0)
\]

Here, \emph{structural resolution increases} (fractal inward),
while \emph{Compton lengths decrease}. This is not a contradiction,
but a \textbf{complementary description} of the same process.

\subsection{Comparison with the standard model}

The standard model says:  
"The space expands, wavelengths are stretched."

FFGFT says:  
"Masses grow, wavelengths shrink, structure becomes finer."

Both descriptions are \textbf{observationally equivalent} (A265),
because \emph{clocks and rulers change together} -- no local
observer can measure the scaling.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The concepts of "minimal length" and "fractal refinement inward" are
\emph{not} a contradiction, but two sides of the same geometric coin:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
	\item \textbf{Length perspective:} from large (antipode) to small (today)
	\item \textbf{Structure perspective:} from coarse (antipode) to fine (today)
\end{itemize}

Both follow from the same mass profile $m(\theta)$ and are consistently
connected in FFGFT.

\textbf{[S]} This clarification makes visible the internal consistency
of FFGFT geometry and resolves a common point of misunderstanding.

\section{Concluding remark: Two opposing models}

\textbf{[S]} An honest acknowledgment belongs at the end.

For us -- as observers with our present masses and clocks
-- our perspective naturally counts. We think in light paths.
We measure redshifts. We experience time as running forward.
This is real in the sense that it is what we measure, and our
instruments are calibrated to it.

The standard model ($\Lambda$CDM) takes exactly this perspective
as its starting point: expanding space, Big Bang as beginning,
redshift as space stretching, CMB as snapshot of an early epoch.
